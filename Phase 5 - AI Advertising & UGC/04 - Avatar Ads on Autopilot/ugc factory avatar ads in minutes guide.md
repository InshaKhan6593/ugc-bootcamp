## What you're building

Use UGC Factory inside Promptwise to create avatar adverts much faster than building every scene manually. The goal is to pick a mode, choose a video type, upload the right product/avatar inputs, generate the clips, then review and assemble the final UGC-style ad.

![](ugc%20factory%20avatar%20ads%20in%20minutes%20guide_images/img_p2_2.jpg)

screenshot: UGC Factory interface showing generation tier selection between Premium (Seedance x Wise UGC) and Standard (Veo 3.1 x Kling UGC)

UGC Factory starts by choosing the generation tier, which controls quality, cost, and how much of the ad is automated.

## 1. Choose Standard or Premium based on the job

UGC Factory starts with two modes. Standard uses fewer credits and is useful when you want a lower-cost route. Premium uses the latest Seedance model for the strongest lip-sync/avatar quality and more automated results.

* Use Standard when credit cost matters.

* Use Premium when you want the highest-quality avatar advert.

* Model names may change over time, but the idea stays the same: Standard is lower-cost, Premium does more of the work.

* Choose based on the quality level and volume of ads you need.

![](ugc%20factory%20avatar%20ads%20in%20minutes%20guide_images/img_p3_2.jpg)

screenshot: UGC Factory interface showing Premium and Standard generation tier cards

The tier cards show the tradeoff between lower-credit Standard generation and higher-quality Premium generation.

## 2. Pick the video type before filling inputs

Premium starts with a grid of video types. This choice controls what inputs you see next and what kind of ad the system builds. The options include talking avatar formats, B-roll formats, product options, fast-cut edits, normal-cut edits, and different content structures.

* Choose the ad structure before adding product details.

* Fast-cut B-roll works well for energetic product ads.

* Talking-avatar options work when the avatar needs to deliver a direct script.

* Voiceover and no-voiceover options change the final ad style.

The video-type grid decides whether the ad becomes talking avatar, fast-cut B-roll, normal B-roll, or another UGC structure.

## 3. Use strong product and avatar inputs

The AI needs enough context to understand the product and who should appear in the ad. Upload the product image, add an inside-product image if packaging hides what it is, then choose or upload an avatar reference when you want better control.

* Upload a clean product image.

* Upload what is inside the packaging if that helps the AI understand usage.

* Upload or choose a premade avatar instead of leaving identity to chance.

* Add product label, aspect ratio, resolution, and product description when needed.

Product and avatar inputs give UGC Factory the context it needs to generate a believable product ad.

## 4. Let the AI write the voiceover when speed matters

If the chosen format includes voiceover, you can paste a custom script or leave it blank. Leaving it blank lets the AI write the ad script for you, which is the fastest route when you just need usable content quickly.

* Use your own script when messaging needs to be exact.

* Leave it blank when speed matters more than control.

* Describe the product clearly so the script has the right angle.

* Review the generated script or voiceover before using it as a final ad.

screenshot: UGC Factory interface showing video generation progress

## 5. Review the generated Premium output

Premium can generate a finished-style UGC ad with product B-roll, avatar shots, and voiceover. This can often be used as an ad with little or no editing, but you still need to check the product, packaging, avatar, lip sync, and pacing.

* Check product accuracy and packaging.

* Check whether the avatar looks believable.

* Check whether B-roll matches the product.

* Check whether the voiceover feels natural and usable.

* If the structure is right, export and test it as an ad.

## 6. Use talking avatar mode for direct lip sync

Talking avatar mode is for direct spoken avatar clips. Upload a good reference, add the line you want the avatar to say, then generate. This is useful for hooks, testimonials, direct claims, UGC intros, and short sales moments.

* Use a strong avatar reference image.

* Keep the spoken line natural.

* Use talking-avatar clips for hooks, product claims, and direct UGC moments.

* Check lip sync, mouth shape, and facial stability before using the clip.

## 7. Use story formats like Problem · Solution

Some templates are designed around storytelling. Problem · Solution works well because it gives the ad emotional structure: the person has a problem, the product appears as the fix, and the final clip shows the outcome.

* Use story templates when the product solves a clear pain point.

* Let the format guide the emotional arc.

* Upload the product so the system can build the story around it.

* Review whether the before and after actually make sense.

Story templates help UGC Factory turn product inputs into a clearer ad structure instead of random product shots.

## 8. Use Standard mode when you want more control per clip

Standard mode is more step-by-step. You choose or select an influencer/avatar, generate a script, then create the hook, body, and call-to-action clips. This is useful when you want to control the structure more manually.

* Choose one influencer or premade avatar first.

* Use the scriptwriter to create the ad script.

* Generate hook, body, and CTA clips separately.

* Use the word counter so Veo dialogue does not speak too fast or become gibberish.

screenshot: Veo interface showing script input and word count warning "Too long for 8s"

![](ugc%20factory%20avatar%20ads%20in%20minutes%20guide_images/img_p10_2.jpg)

Standard mode breaks the ad into clips, which gives more manual control over hook, body, and CTA generation.

**Standard mode warning**

Keep each dialogue clip within the recommended word count. Too many or too few words can make Veo speak too fast or produce broken speech.

## 9. Add B-roll and assemble the final ad

Since short avatar clips may need to be stitched together, export the generated parts and assemble them in CapCut. B-roll can sit over the talking avatar clips, add product context, and create new camera angles.

* Generate hook, body, and CTA clips.

* Generate supporting B-roll from product images and premade options.

* Download the pieces.

* Edit everything together in CapCut.

* Use B-roll to make the ad feel less static and more polished.

## The big idea

UGC Factory is the shortcut for avatar adverts. Premium does more of the work automatically and usually gives the strongest result. Standard gives more step-by-step control and uses fewer credits. Either way, the workflow is the same: choose the structure, add the right inputs, generate, review, and assemble the strongest outputs into an ad.

* Use Premium when speed and quality matter most.

* Use Standard when cost and manual control matter more.

* Upload strong product and avatar references.

* Check lip sync, product accuracy, pacing, and B-roll before using the ad.

* Finish in CapCut when clips need stitching or extra polish.
