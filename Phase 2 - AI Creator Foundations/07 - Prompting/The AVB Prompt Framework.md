## Why Prompting Actually Matters

*Prompting is direction, not just description.*

This is one of the most important skills in the course because once you understand prompting properly, AI stops feeling random. It stops feeling like you are typing words into a box and hoping the machine gives you something good.

Most of the time, the difference between average AI content and professional-looking AI content is not the tool, luck, or some secret model. It is the instruction. The same model can create something flat, fake, plastic and forgettable, or something cinematic, emotional, realistic and scroll-stopping. The only thing that changes is how clearly you communicate what you want.

For images, your prompt tells the AI what the frame should look like. For video, your prompt tells the AI what should move, what the camera should do, and what should happen in the shot.

**The less direction you give, the more the AI has to fill in the blanks. And when AI fills in the blanks, it usually chooses the most average version of the idea.**

## Bad Prompt vs Better Prompt

*A good prompt is not just longer. It is more intentional.*

**Bad prompt:**

*A cool photo of a girl.*

This might still generate something decent, but nothing in that prompt gives the AI real direction. Who is she? Where is she? What is she doing? What is the lighting? What is the camera angle? Is this supposed to feel candid, cinematic, luxurious, emotional or realistic? The AI has to guess everything.

**Better prompt:**

*A candid photo of a woman in her late twenties with short dark hair wearing an oversized denim jacket, sitting at the counter of a tiny ramen shop in Tokyo late at night. Steam rises from the bowl in front of her, warm overhead light hits the side of her face. The background is softly blurred with shelves of bowls and handwritten menus, shot from across the counter like an intimate documentary photo, quiet and cozy mood, realistic 35mm film look.*

That second prompt gives the AI a subject, location, time of day, light source, camera position, background details, emotional tone and visual style. Now the model has something useful to work with.

# The 6-Part AVB Prompt Framework

*Use this as your creative compass.*

From now on, use this framework for images, video, ads, cinematic scenes, product shots, AI influencers, thumbnails, UGC and almost everything else you create.

*Whenever a prompt is not working, it is usually because one or more of these six parts is missing, weak or unclear.*

## 1. Subject

Who or what is in the frame?

## 2. Environment

Where is this happening?

## 3. Camera

How are we seeing it?

## 4. Lighting

What is the light source?

## 5. Mood

How should it feel?

## 6. Style

What should the final look be?

<!-- page 5 -->

## 1. Subject

*Who or what is in the frame?*

* For a person: age, hair, clothing, expression, posture and what they are doing.

* For a product: shape, material, color, surface texture, branding style, packaging and position.

* If the subject is vague, the whole output becomes vague.

**Example direction:**

*A man in his early 40s with tired eyes, short messy brown hair, light stubble, wearing a worn black leather jacket, holding a takeaway coffee, standing still like he is waiting for someone.*

<!-- page 6 -->

## 2. Environment

*Where is this happening?*

* Use the environment to create context, atmosphere and story.

* Include a specific place, time of day, weather, background objects, textures and atmosphere.

* A clear environment makes AI content feel more expensive, realistic and original.

**Example direction:**

*On a narrow side street in Hong Kong at night, neon signs stacked above small food stalls, scooters parked along the pavement, steam rising from a noodle cart, wet ground reflecting the lights.*

<!-- page 7 -->

## 3. Camera

*How are we seeing it?*

* The camera decides how the viewer experiences the image.

* Wide shots build the world. Medium shots feel natural for social content. Close-ups focus on emotion and texture.

* For video, camera becomes movement: push in, handheld follow, slow orbit, static tripod shot.

**Example direction:**

*A medium close-up shot of a businessman sitting alone in a luxury hotel lobby, camera positioned slightly below eye level across the table, background softly blurred.*

<!-- page 8 -->

## 4. Lighting

*What is the light source?*

* Do not just write cinematic lighting. Describe where the light comes from.

* Think source, direction and quality: window light, street lights, phone screens, candles, overhead lights, car headlights.

* Bad lighting is one of the fastest ways to get fake plastic AI results.

**Example direction:**

*A small warm table lamp on the left side of the frame lighting her face, while the rest of the room falls into soft shadow.*

<!-- page 9 -->

## 5. Mood

### How should it feel?

* Mood is the emotional direction of the image.

* You usually only need one or two mood words.

* Mood influences expression, color, contrast, atmosphere, framing and body language.

### Example direction:

*Quiet and nostalgic. Tense and paranoid. Cozy and intimate. Cold and isolated. Luxurious and calm.*

<!-- page 10 -->

## 6. Style

*What should the final look be?*

* Style is optional. Do not over-style if you want realism.

* Use style when you want a specific treatment: iPhone flash photo, Polaroid, clean studio product photography, anime, oil painting, dark fantasy.

* Words like cinematic masterpiece, hyper-detailed, ultra-glossy and perfect lighting can push the image into fake AI territory.

**Example direction:**

*Realistic 35mm film photo. iPhone flash photo. Clean studio product photography. Luxury fashion campaign. Dark fantasy style.*

## The Simple Prompt Recipe

*Use this when you are building a new image prompt from scratch.*

**[Subject] in [environment], [camera angle or framing], [lighting source and direction], [mood], [visual style].**

**Example:**

*A candid photo of a tired chef in his early 50s wiping down the counter of a tiny ramen shop in Tokyo after midnight, medium close-up from across the counter, warm overhead light reflecting off stainless steel, quiet and exhausted mood, realistic 35mm documentary photo.*

**For video, add movement:**

Start with the image prompt, then describe what moves. The camera slowly pushes in. Steam rises from the bowl. He looks down, exhales, then glances toward the door. Keep the movement simple and specific.

## Quick Prompt Checklist

*Before you generate, check the six parts.*

*

Is the subject specific enough? [ ]
\*

Do I know where the scene is happening? [ ]
\*

Did I tell the AI how the camera sees the scene? [ ]
\*

Did I describe where the light is coming from? [ ]
\*

Did I choose one or two mood words? [ ]
\*

Did I add style only if it actually helps? [ ]

***If a result looks generic, do not blame the model first. Check which part of the framework was missing or unclear. That is usually where the fix is.***
