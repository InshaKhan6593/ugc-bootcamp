## What you're building

Use this guide to understand the four main types of AI models used inside an AI content workflow. The goal is to stop thinking of AI as one giant tool and start thinking of it like a workshop: different models do different jobs, and the best results come from using the right model at the right stage.

* Chat models help you think, plan, write, structure, brainstorm, and troubleshoot.

* Image models turn text prompts or references into still visuals.

* Video models turn text, images, or references into moving clips.

* Voice and audio models add narration, dialogue, sound effects, and music.

<!-- page 3 -->

## 1. Think of AI models like specialist tools

An AI model is software trained to do a specific job. It has learned patterns from huge amounts of examples, which means different models become good at different tasks. One model might be better at realistic faces. Another might be better at cinematic video. Another might be built for voice, sound, or text.

The easiest way to understand this is to think of a workshop. A saw, drill, sander, and paint sprayer are all useful, but you would not use them for the same job. AI models work the same way. The skill is knowing which tool to reach for and when.

Simple mental model

`Do not ask: Which AI model is best?`
`Ask: What job do I need the model to do right now?`

## 2. Chat models: the planning layer

Chat models are text-based AI systems. ChatGPT is the one most people know, but there are others like Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek, and Wise inside Promptwise. These models are best for thinking, writing, planning, and strategy.

* Use chat models when you are stuck for ideas.

* Use them to write image prompts and video prompts.

* Use them to write scripts, hooks, outlines, captions, and content plans.

* Use them to troubleshoot why a creative result is not working.

* Use them to plan a full workflow before opening an image or video tool.

Think of the chat model as a creative director that works 24/7. Most people use it to write basic emails. Inside AVB, the better use is building the entire creative workflow: idea, script, prompt, variation plan, and improvement loop.

## 3. Image models: the visual foundation

Image models take a text description, reference image, or prompt and turn it into a still image. This is where a lot of AI workflows begin, because the image often becomes the foundation for the video.

* Use image models for characters, avatars, influencers, product shots, thumbnails, ads, and cinematic frames.

* Different image models have different strengths, so choosing the right one matters.

* Some are better at realistic humans, skin, and faces.

* Some are better at stylized concepts, abstract visuals, or art direction.

* If you can describe an idea clearly, you can usually start producing it visually.

The key point is not just that image models exist. The key point is that they become the visual foundation for later steps. A strong image gives the video model something much stronger to animate.

<!-- page 6 -->

## 4. Video models: the motion layer

Video models create moving clips from text, images, references, start frames, end frames, or other inputs. This is where static ideas become motion, camera movement, character movement, product movement, or cinematic scenes.

* Some video models are better for cinematic, high-quality output.

* Some are better for lip sync or speaking avatars.

* Some handle human motion and faces better than others.

* Some are better for fast experimentation, while others are better for final-quality shots.

AI video has moved far beyond the obvious AI slop from last year. The opportunity comes from the fact that video models are now realistic enough for ads, social content, cinematic scenes, product demos, and branded creative.

<!-- page 7 -->

## 5. Voice and audio models: the immersion layer

Voice and audio models handle everything sound-related: voiceover, narration, voice cloning, dialogue, sound effects, music, and background atmosphere.

* Use voice models for narration, character dialogue, explainer voiceovers, and ad reads.

* Use sound effects to make video feel physical and immersive.

* Use music to control pacing, mood, and energy.

* Layering audio after the video can make the final result feel much more finished.

A video can look good and still feel weak if the sound is empty. Audio is what makes the scene feel alive. In ads and filmmaking phases, sound is often the difference between something that feels generated and something that feels produced.
