# AI VIDEO BOOTCAMP

## The AI Creator Workflow

The Complete Step-by-Step Process to Create AI Content That Actually Looks Good

<!-- page 2 -->

Up until now, we have talked about tools, chats, prompts, models and ideas. But none of that really matters if you do not have a process. The process is what turns random AI experimenting into actual creation. It is what stops you from sitting there generating twenty random things, wasting credits, getting frustrated, and wondering why nothing looks as good as the examples you see from other people.

Now, this workflow is the foundation, but during this course we will teach you tools on Promptwise that can multiply how fast this workflow is done, including using Flows, which is our node based creation system. Tools and advancements like this in 2026 will allow you to create so much faster and move from Idea to full post in half the time, so bear that in mind.

Most beginners think AI creation is simple: type a prompt, get a result, post it. But that is not the real workflow. That is one small part of the workflow. In fact, generating is step three of a six-step process.

The people who consistently make strong AI content are not just lucky. They are not magically better at the tools. They follow a process. They know what they are making before they open the generator. They use chat to develop the idea before spending credits. They start with images before jumping into video. They refine the result before adding motion. Then they edit, package and actually ship the final piece.

This workflow applies whether you are making a TikTok, an AI influencer post, a product image, a UGC ad, a YouTube thumbnail, a cinematic short, a client asset, or a full story-based video. The complexity changes depending on the project, but the process stays the same.

## The Workflow

1: Think of a basic idea or general concept you want to create

2: Wise / ChatGPT / Claude / any AI chat for Improved concept, Scriptwriting, Prompts and Idea planning

3: Chatgpt Image 2.0 / Nanobanana Pro / Midjourney etc for Image Generation (Promptwise)

4: Image Refinement and Upscaling

5: Seedance, Kling, Veo, Gemini Omni etc for AI Video Generation (Promptwise)

6: CapCut for Editing, Add Sound Effects and Post

*Use this order of workflow when producing AI content.*

*Idea → Chat → Image → Refine → Video → Edit & Post*

## 1. 📌 IDEA — Define The Outcome

Goal: Define what your video or content is about and who it is for.

The first step is idea. What are you making, and why?

This sounds obvious, but most people skip it. They sit down and think, "I want to make something cool." But that is not an idea. That is just a vague desire. And vague ideas create vague prompts, vague prompts create average outputs, and average outputs create content nobody really cares about.

A proper idea is specific:

"I'm making a 15-second cinematic TikTok of a samurai standing in heavy rain for my fantasy content page."

"I'm creating a product photo of a perfume bottle in a luxury bathroom setting for a client's Instagram."

"I'm making a 30-second UGC-style ad for a skincare brand using a hook, problem, solution and CTA structure."

"I'm building a new character for my AI influencer account."

Those are clear outcomes. You know what you are making, where it is going, who it is for, and what the asset needs to do.

This step matters because the clearer the outcome is, the easier every other step becomes. If I know I am making a 9:16 TikTok of an AI influencer, I already know the aspect ratio, the subject, the environment, the mood, the platform and the basic visual direction before I have even opened a tool.

**Before you generate anything, answer these questions:**

* What am I making?

* Where is it going?

* Who is it for?

* What should it make people feel?

* What does the final output need to be? A still image, a video, an ad, a thumbnail, a post, a portfolio piece, or a client asset?

*If you cannot answer those questions, you are not ready to generate yet. Spend two minutes defining the outcome here. It will save you time later.*

## 2. ✍ CHAT — Plan and Write

*Tool: Wise / ChatGPT / Claude / any AI chat*

**Goal:** Develop your idea, build the prompts, structure the shot list and make the output stronger before you generate anything. This is where you take a general concept and actually make it useable.

Once you know what you are making, the next step is chat. This is where AI chats becomes your creative assistant.

Check the AI chat's lesson & PDF's for the best way to talk to AI chat about your ideas.

**For an AI influencer post, you could say:**

*"I'm creating a realistic AI influencer image for Instagram. Help me develop the setting, outfit, pose, camera style and realism details so it feels like a real candid photo, not a glossy AI model shoot."*

Ask it to improve the first answer. Ask what is weak. Ask what might make the output look fake. Ask it to rewrite the prompt with more natural lighting, better composition, stronger realism or a clearer subject.

LLM chats need to become your best friend if you want to keep up. Not because they magically do everything for you, but because they help you think, plan and improve faster.

## 3. 🎨 IMAGE — Generate Your Visuals

*Tool: Promptwise for Image Generation*

Goal: Generate your first visual using the prompt and direction you built in chat.

At this point, you are not just randomly typing into an image model. You have a clear outcome from Step 1 and a stronger prompt from Step 2. That means you are already ahead of most people.

Pick the right model for the job, set the correct aspect ratio, paste in your prompt and generate your first visual.

*The important principle here is this: start with images, not video.*

Even if your final output is a video, you should usually begin with an image. Images are cheaper, faster and easier to control. They allow you to lock in the composition, character, setting, lighting and overall feel before you move into the more expensive video stage.

A lot of people jump straight into video and then wonder why the result is a mess. The character is wrong, the framing is wrong, the lighting is wrong, the environment is not what they imagined, and now they are trying to fix all of that inside a video model. That is the expensive way to create.

The smarter way is to use the image stage as your foundation and then turn it into video.

**When you get your image, ask proper quality-control questions:**

* Is this consistent with all the other scenes that are going in my video?

* Does the lighting match the mood?

* Does the person, product or character look believable?

* Is the aspect ratio correct for the platform?

* Does it look real enough, or does it have that obvious AI gloss and plastic look?

If the answer is no, do not move forward yet. Either regenerate with a better prompt or go back to chat and ask Wise what to fix.

Remember, the image is the base. If the base is bad, everything after it will be too.

## 4. 📥 REFINE — Edit and Upscale

*Tool: Promptwise Studio / Canva for Image Refinement and Upscaling*

Goal: Fix specific issues and upscale to higher resolution before moving to video.

Once you have an image that is close, the next step is refinement. This is where a lot of creators separate themselves from beginners.

Beginners accept the first decent result. Serious creators look at the image and ask, "What would make this better?"

If the image is 90% there, edit it using models like GPT image 2.0 or Nanobanana Pro. Use editing tools to fix specific issues:

* Maybe the background needs changing

* Maybe the lighting needs adjusting

* Maybe the hands are slightly off

* Maybe the outfit is not right

* Maybe the product label needs to be clearer

* Maybe the face looks too smooth

* Maybe one detail is ruining an otherwise strong image

This is the stage where you fix those things before moving into video. You can use Canva here for making any small changes too.

If the image needs upscaling, which means increasing its resolution or quality, do it now before you turn it into a video. The higher resolution an image is, the better it will look when you turn it into video because the AI software has more pixels to work with.

**But there is an important rule:**

*If the composition is wrong, the character is wrong, the concept is wrong, the camera angle is wrong, or the whole thing feels off, go back to Step 2. Fix the prompt and regenerate. If the image is fundamentally wrong, do not edit it. Do not waste time polishing a bad foundation.*

## 5. 🎥 VIDEO — Add Motion

*Tool: Promptwise for AI Video Generation*

**Goal:** Bring your refined images to life with motion and camera effects.

Once your image is strong, refined and ready, then you move into video. This is where you add motion to the visual.

You take your final image and use a video model to bring it to life. The mistake a lot of people make here is that they re-describe the entire image in the video prompt. You usually do not need to do that. The model can already see the image.

Your video prompt should focus on what motion you want to see:

* • Camera movement (dolly, zoom, tilt, pan, push-in)

* • Subject movement (turns head, walks forward, picks up object)

* • Environmental motion (wind in hair, rain falling, waves moving)

* • Lighting changes (sun shifting, shadows moving)

We will go much deeper into video prompting in Phase 4.

*And remember, if your final output is a still image, you can skip this step entirely. Not everything needs to become video.*

## 6. 🎞️ EDIT & POST — Finish and ship

*Tool: CapCut for Editing, Pixabay / Elevenlabs for Sound Effects*

**Goal:** Edit your final video together and publish it.

The final step is editing and posting. This is where everything becomes a finished piece of content.

Bring your clips, images, voiceover, music, sound effects, captions, overlays or text into CapCut or whatever editor you prefer. Some people use Premier.

The pacing, sound, captions, transitions, export settings and final packaging all affect how the content feels when someone actually watches **it.**

<!-- page 9 -->

**If it is a TikTok, Reel or Short:**

* Make sure the first second is strong

* Add captions if they help the viewer follow the idea

* Use sound design to make the scene feel alive

* Add ambient audio: footsteps, rain, crowd noise, fabric movement, camera sounds, whatever fits the piece

* Apply transitions, pacing and music that match the energy

* Shorten or lengthen scenes as needed

## The Full Workflow

That is the main AI Creator Workflow:

*Idea* → *Chat* → *Image* → *Refine* → *Video* → *Editing, Sound Effects & Posting*

Six steps even if the concept is simple or complex. You might use this workflow for one image, one TikTok, one AI influencer post, one UGC ad, or a full short film. The scale changes, but the process stays the same.
