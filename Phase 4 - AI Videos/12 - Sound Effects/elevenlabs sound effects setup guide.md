# ElevenLabs Voiceovers and Sound Effects Setup

Lesson Support Guide

<!-- page 2 -->

## What you're building

Use this guide to set up AI voiceovers and sound effects for your videos. The goal is to make the final video feel more immersive, realistic, and produced by adding voices, sound effects, and music at the right stage of the edit.

Visuals can carry the idea, but audio makes the scene feel alive. A good voiceover, realistic sound design, and well-placed effects can make AI video feel much more believable.

<!-- page 3 -->

## 1. What ElevenLabs is used for

ElevenLabs is an AI voice platform that can generate realistic human-like voices. It can also generate sound effects. Use it when you need narration, character dialogue, ad voiceovers, faceless content, or immersive audio elements.

* Use voiceovers to add narration or realism.

* Use generated voices when you do not want to record your own voice.

* Use sound effects to make a scene feel physical and immersive.

* Use audio after the visual edit is mostly built so you know where each sound belongs.

<!-- page 4 -->

## 2. Choose the right voice

Inside ElevenLabs, go to the Voices tab and choose a voice that fits the project. Different voices create different levels of trust, energy, age, tone, accent, and professionalism.

* Rachel can work for a neutral American-style voice.

* Adam can work for a deeper, more professional tone.

* Choose the voice based on the project, not just because it sounds impressive.

* A calm educational video needs a different voice than a high-energy ad.

Once you pick a voice, press Use and paste your script into the script box.

<!-- page 5 -->

## 3. Write the voiceover script properly

ElevenLabs can handle up to around 5000 characters in one go, which is enough for a lot of short-form voiceovers. The writing still matters. The more naturally the script is written, the more natural the voice will sound.

* Use commas and punctuation to guide pacing.

* Write how someone would actually speak, not like a stiff essay.

* Break long thoughts into shorter lines if the delivery feels rushed.

* Adjust the voice settings if the tone or speed feels off.

**Voiceover writing tip**

Punctuation is direction. Commas, full stops, and shorter phrases tell the AI where to breathe and slow down.

<!-- page 6 -->

## 4. Generate, listen, and adjust

After pasting the script, adjust the available settings if needed, then press Generate. Listen back before downloading. If the voice sounds too flat, rushed, robotic, or unnatural, adjust the script and settings rather than accepting the first version.

* Play it back before downloading.

* Adjust pacing with punctuation.

* Change the speed if the delivery feels too fast or too slow.

* Try a different voice if the tone does not fit the project.

<!-- page 7 -->

## 5. Use voice changer when needed

Voice changer is useful for faceless content or when you do not want to use your own voice. You can upload recorded audio or a video, choose a different voice, and generate a changed version.

* Use it if you do not like how your voice sounds in a video.

* Use it if you want to stay anonymous.

* Use it to match a different character, narrator, or content style.

* Voice cloning exists too, but it is not needed for most AI videos and can be saved for more advanced workflows.

<!-- page 8 -->

## 6. Generate sound effects

Sound effects are what make the viewer feel like they are inside the video. Open the sound effects section, choose a category or type in the sound you need, and generate samples.

* Set the duration to around 4 or 5 seconds for most effects.

* Type the exact sound you need, such as ocean waves, footsteps, camera shutter, city ambience, rain, door creak, crowd noise, or engine rumble.

* Generate multiple samples and choose the one that fits the scene best.

* Do not use sounds just because they are available. Use sounds that match the action and environment.

**Example**

`If the video is on a boat, generate or download ocean waves, water movement, wind, rope creaks, and subtle boat ambience.`

<!-- page 9 -->

## 7. Use Promptwise or Pixabay for audio assets

If ElevenLabs is included inside your Promptwise subscription, you can use the audio tab there instead of paying separately through the ElevenLabs website. That keeps the workflow simpler.

Pixabay is another useful source for free music and sound effects. Search for the sound you need, download it, then bring it into your edit. Pixabay assets are copyright free, which makes them useful for course projects, social content, and client work.

<!-- page 10 -->

## 8. Add sound at the end of the edit

Sound design usually works best near the end of the video process. Finish the visual edit first, then add voiceover, sound effects, music, and ambience once you know exactly where each sound needs to land.

* Import your voiceover and sound effects into CapCut or your editor.

* Layer the sounds to match the timing of the visuals.

* Use subtle ambience underneath the main action when needed.

* Keep sound effects natural. If they are too loud or too obvious, they can make the video feel cheap.

The big idea: audio should support the world of the video. Voice gives information, music gives emotion, and sound effects make the scene feel physical.
