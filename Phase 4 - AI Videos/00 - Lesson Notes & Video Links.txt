02. 💫 Which Video Models To Use
--------------------------------------------------
Lesson page: https://www.skool.com/aivideobootcamp/classroom/049622b6?md=9932d788591d44dfbc8ddf9efad37b84

When choosing an AI video model, don’t think, “Which one is best?”
Think, “What am I trying to make?”
Seedance 2.0 is by far the best all round model right now, but it is also the most expensive. If you have the budget and want the best results, it's quite far ahead of the rest.
On Seedance you can upload up to 9 image, 3 videos & 3 Audio as references, at the same time.
[Image 1: https://assets.skool.com/f/1a18982c15714b0c97031894dbdb3900/9be7b88645f342559f6de1d07149f14cbc2d1065bade4eb280fccc44b85f6ded.png]

Kling 3.0 4k is great for B-roll and slower paced Cinematic shots, and if you need to control the direction of the movement more precisely, use Kling Motion Control.
Google Gemini Omni is great for lip-sync and video editing. Use this as a back-up to Seedance when you want great lip-sync.
Veo 3.1 is good for cheaper lip-sync and scenes, but is not on the same level as Seedance or Gemini Omni.
Veo 3.1 has 2 settings, Quality or Fast. Quality costs more credits but gives a slightly better output.
Heygen is the best at creating clones and doing 'Talking Head' Videos. Look at Phase 9 for info on cloning yourself.
For quick tests, use cheaper or faster models. Once the idea works, regenerate the best version with a higher-quality model.

## The simple rule is this:
• Overall best for Avatars - Seedance 2.0
• Overall best for B-roll/Cinematic slow shots - Kling 3.0
• Overall great at most things - Google Gemini Omni
• Overall best for Cloning & Talking head - Heygen

10. 🎙️ Consistent Character Voices
--------------------------------------------------
Lesson page: https://www.skool.com/aivideobootcamp/classroom/049622b6?md=b0f04241197947dd818a63fbd46392ee

Veo:
The trick with keeping voices consistent in Veo 3.1 is basically treating the voice like a locked character asset, not something you describe fresh every time.
Where most people mess up is they'll tweak the voice description between prompts. Even tiny changes, like swapping a word or two, can make the model give you a completely different tone or pitch. 
It's annoying but that's how it works. What you want to do instead is define the voice once with really specific traits. 
This also works in other VIDEO MODELS!

## Examples: Accent, tone, pacing, personality. 

Something like: "light Australian accent, relaxed and confident, medium pace, bit of vocal rasp." 

Then copy paste that exact description into every single prompt. Basically think of it as a voice blueprint. Define it once and just reuse it across every scene (for that same character)

### Also, keep your dialogue lines short and natural. This helps the avatar stay in rhythm. If you upload too much dialogue in each prompt, the avatar only has 8 seconds to say all of those words, so they are going to speak super fast, which doesn't sound natural at all.
Here's a good example:
[Image 1: https://assets.skool.com/f/1a18982c15714b0c97031894dbdb3900/804722ac0e3b455a9626ad144a326b65315080ae798143e58ede54e4d7d44862-md.png]

## Why default AI voices sound bad
Standard AI delivery treats every single word the same, there's no emphasis on the words that matter, no natural pauses where a real person would breathe, no melody in how sentences rise and fall, your brain picks up on this immediately. 
In the prompt above I add "..." and punctuation quite often. This let's the AI know that it needs to pause naturally between speech. The more punctuation the better, it gives the prompt rhythm.
If you don't add any sort of punctuation it will just sound like they are reading from a script, which is bad.
Then we finish the prompt with the "light Australian accent, relaxed and confident, medium pace, bit of vocal rasp." which locks in the tone and sound of the voice.

Why are accents important?
When you add an accent, you're changing the entire delivery system. These are tiny micro details that your ear processes subconsciously. Even a subtle accent is enough to transform a flat robotic voice into something that actually sounds like a real person talking. 
Think about it, there is probably only around 2-3 different female voices in Veo 3.1 for an australian accent (or any accent), so it's quite limited. 
This is a good thing, because it means that if you choose your accent, then the software doesn't have much choice but to continually give you the same sort of voice.
REMEMBER - YOU NEED TO CHOOSE AN ACCENT THAT MATCHES YOUR CHARACTER.

## Some examples you can use (add the accent you want):

### Conversational
"speaks with a subtle [accent], natural mid-tempo pacing, friendly and relaxed tone. briefly pauses before key words as if thinking. sentences taper off slightly rather than ending sharply."

### Confident
"light [accent], steady deliberate delivery, calm authority in the voice. controlled pacing with clear pauses between statements. never rushed, every phrase lands with intention."

### Energetic
"subtle [accent], fast lively rhythm, enthusiasm rises when emphasizing a point. delivery feels animated and expressive, like someone excitedly sharing a story."

### Casual
"light [accent], loose conversational pace. sounds like talking with a close friend. occasionally breaks sentences, uses natural filler sounds like 'um' or 'like'. tone is relaxed and informal."

### Storytelling
"light [accent], expressive storytelling cadence. pacing varies naturally — slower during description, faster during exciting moments. voice carries curiosity and subtle emotional shifts."

### Calm / Thoughtful
"subtle [accent], slower reflective pace. soft tone with gentle pauses between ideas. sounds like someone carefully choosing their words while explaining something meaningful."

### Persuasive
"light [accent], confident persuasive rhythm. slightly emphasises important words, small pauses before key statements. tone feels convincing and composed."

### Professional
"subtle [accent], clean controlled delivery, medium pace. clear pronunciation with slight pauses between sentences. tone feels polished and articulate."

### Dramatic
"light [accent], deliberate cinematic pacing. longer pauses for tension, emphasis on emotional words. voice carries weight and seriousness."

### Curious / Analytical
"subtle [accent], thoughtful pace with slight hesitation before ideas. tone sounds inquisitive, as if exploring the topic while speaking."

### Friendly / Warm
"light [accent], gentle rhythm, soft welcoming tone. speech flows smoothly with small pauses that feel natural and reassuring."

### Sarcastic / Playful
"subtle [accent], relaxed tempo with slight vocal smirks in the delivery. emphasis shifts unpredictably to highlight humor or irony."

### Authority / Leader
"light [accent], controlled confident cadence. speaks slowly enough to command attention. strong emphasis on decisive words."

### Reflective / Emotional
"subtle [accent], slower pacing with pauses that feel introspective. voice softens during emotional phrases, almost like thinking out loud."

### Conversational Story Mode
"light [accent], casual narrative rhythm. sounds like someone explaining an experience to a friend. sentences occasionally restart mid thought."

### Fast Social Media Style
"subtle [accent], quick modern pacing, energetic delivery. short bursts of speech with strong emphasis on key words."
