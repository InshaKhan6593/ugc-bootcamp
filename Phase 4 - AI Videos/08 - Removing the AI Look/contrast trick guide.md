# AI VIDEO BOOTCAMP

## Contrast Trick

Lesson Support Guide

<!-- page 2 -->

## What you're building

Use this guide to make AI video footage feel less polished, less fake, and more like real phone footage. The goal is to remove some of the perfect AI finish so UGC-style ads, iPhone-style clips, and realistic social videos feel more believable.

Real phone footage is not perfect. It has softer detail, flatter colour, imperfect movement, tiny hand shake, and a more casual feel. AI footage often comes out too clean, too contrasty, too saturated, and too stable. That is what gives it away.

<!-- page 3 -->

## 1. Understand the problem with raw AI footage

When AI video comes straight out of a generator, it often has obvious tells. The contrast can be too strong, the saturation can feel too clean, and the whole clip can look like a high-end render instead of something filmed casually on a phone.

* Too much contrast makes the footage feel overly punchy and produced.

* Too much saturation makes the colours feel too perfect and digital.

* Overly stable camera movement makes the clip feel generated instead of handheld.

* The cleaner the footage looks, the less native it can feel for social ads.

This matters most for UGC ads, iPhone-style content, realistic TikTok clips, and social videos where the goal is to feel native instead of cinematic.

<!-- page 4 -->

## 2. Start with colour correction

The first fix is colour. Add an adjustment layer or adjustment filter in your editor, then use that layer to soften the overall image. Do not try to make the clip look worse. The goal is to make it feel less artificially perfect.

* Bring the contrast down heavily enough that the image stops feeling like a render.

* Lower saturation slightly so the colour feels less clean and digital.

* Raise highlights a little if the image feels too dense or heavy.

* Keep checking the before and after so you do not overcorrect.

<!-- page 5 -->

## 3. Lower contrast first

Contrast is usually the biggest giveaway. AI clips often have a very punchy, high-contrast look. That can look impressive at first, but it also makes the clip feel produced and fake.

Drop the contrast until the image starts to feel softer. You are looking for the point where it stops looking like a perfect AI render and starts feeling closer to normal camera footage.

Editing note

`Do not crush the image until it looks washed out. Lower the contrast enough to remove the AI polish, but keep the clip usable.`

<!-- page 6 -->

## 4. Reduce saturation slightly

After contrast, bring the saturation down a little. The keyword is slightly. You do not want to destroy the colour or make the footage look grey. You just want to take the digital edge off it.

* If colours look too rich, pull saturation down.

* If skin looks too perfect or glossy, reduce the colour intensity.

* If the clip starts looking lifeless, you have gone too far.

<!-- page 7 -->

## 5. Lift highlights carefully

Raising the highlights slightly can soften the shot and make the lighting feel more natural. This can help when the AI footage feels too dense, too heavy, or too cinematic in a fake way.

Do not blow the shot out. A small lift is enough. The goal is a subtle camera-footage feel, not a broken exposure.

<!-- page 8 -->

## 6. Add subtle handheld movement

AI clips can feel fake because the camera is too stable. Real phone footage usually has tiny imperfections. When someone holds a phone, their hands naturally move a little.

In CapCut, you can add a shake effect, but the default will usually be way too intense. Turn it down until the movement is barely noticeable. If the shake becomes obvious, it stops feeling real and starts looking like an effect.

**Shake rule**

`The viewer should feel the camera is handheld, but they should not notice the shake effect. If they notice it, reduce it.`

<!-- page 9 -->

## 7. Use this as a realism pass

This technique is simple, but it can save AI footage that would otherwise feel unusable. Think of it as a realism pass after generation: soften the colour, reduce the AI polish, add tiny movement, and make the clip feel more like something a person actually filmed.

* Start with a strong reference image before generating the video.

* Generate the clip in Seedance or your chosen video model.

* Apply the realism pass in editing.

* Compare before and after so you can see whether the footage feels more natural.

* Use this most often for UGC, phone footage, social clips, and casual ad formats.

The big idea: realistic AI footage is not always about making the generation sharper. Sometimes it becomes more believable when you make it softer, flatter, less perfect, and slightly more human.
