# Avatar product photos and product visuals

Use when a person must hold, wear or show a product accurately.

## Products & AI chat

### What you·re building

Use this workflow to turn basic product references into polished product visuals, avatar product photos, ad images, and brand content using image generators plus AI chat. You do not need a studio, photographer, props, locations, or a full creative team when you give the AI strong references and clear direction.

Lesson opener: Products & AI chat.

## The workflow at a glance

* Start with the product, model, or inspiration image you want to work from.

* Use an AI chat to help write a detailed image prompt instead of guessing inside the generator.

* Upload both the product/reference image and the prompt into the image generator.

* Generate multiple options using the latest image models, then pick the strongest result.

* Keep editing the winning image by adding accessories, changing angles, changing the setting, or improving the product placement.

* Use AI chat when you want the chat, references, canvas, and generation workflow in one place.

AI chat view: prompt, uploaded references, AI response, and generated canvas outputs in one workspace.

## 1. Use AI chat before image generation

Start with a simple fashion example: one model image and one product image, like a branded shirt. Instead of writing a weak one-line prompt, send both images to an AI chat and ask it to write the exact image-editing prompt you need. This gives the image model more context and usually creates a more realistic result.

This matters most when the product has details, labels, logos, textures, or patterns. Short prompts can work, but longer and more specific prompts are more reliable when consistency matters.

Generated fashion/product examples: the model and product references are used to create a believable edited result.

## 2. Keep editing the strongest result

Once you get a good image, do not treat it as finished immediately. You can drag the result back into the prompt bar, or download it and reuse it with the AI chat. From there, add details like necklaces, accessories, different angles, wider framing, studio context, or extra brand elements.

* Use the latest image models whenever possible.

* If the product has text, include that text in the prompt.

* If a product pattern matters, describe it clearly and provide a strong reference.

* If the first output is close but not perfect, iterate instead of starting over.

Prompt-writing example: use the chat to produce a more detailed instruction before generating.

## 3. Use Pinterest-style inspiration without copying

Use product imagery from Pinterest as inspiration, but do not directly copy campaign images. Look at what makes the visual work, then rebuild the idea with your own product, model, colors, and details.

A useful prompt pattern is: describe the inspiration image, explain which product should replace the original product, include the product label text, and tell the AI what should change so the final output is original.

## 4. Why AI chat is faster

AI chat works like a normal AI chat, but it also has a built-in canvas, image references, backend prompt knowledge, and image models inside the same workspace. That means you can upload references, write your instruction, and generate images without constantly copying prompts and re-uploading files elsewhere.

* Each chat has its own canvas, so outputs stay organized by project.

* References uploaded in the chat can automatically carry into the generation workflow.

* The Generate button creates images directly inside the chat canvas.

* You can use the @ symbol to reference images already inside your canvas.

* You can reuse outputs as references and continue building from them.

## 5. Avatar product photos

One of the most useful product formats is the avatar product photo: a believable person holding the product directly toward the camera. A product by itself can work, but a product being held by a realistic person instantly feels more native, more social, and more usable for ads.

The best workflow is to create the avatar first, then use the avatar image and the product image together as references. If you only type a prompt like "make the person hold the bottle," the model often gets the product wrong, makes the label blurry, or changes the scale.

* Generate or choose the avatar first so you control the look of the person.

* Upload the avatar and product image into the chat or generator together.

* Include product label text and product details in the prompt.

* Use multiple product references when size and proportions matter.

* Check whether the product label, scale, hands, and angle look believable before animating.

## Common mistakes to avoid

* Using a vague prompt and expecting the model to guess the label, texture, product scale, and composition.

* Uploading a product image but not describing the label text in the prompt.

* Trying to make the product appear in someone's hand without first creating a strong avatar reference.

* Copying inspiration images too directly instead of changing the subject, background, colors, and product details.

* Accepting the first result even when a small prompt improvement would make the image more usable.

## Big idea

AI product visuals work best when you treat the image model like a production assistant, not a magic button. Give it the product, the person, the inspiration, the label text, the size context, and the job of the image. The more clearly you direct the scene, the more believable the final ad visual becomes.

## Chat prompts from the lesson video

**To get the avatar-product prompt from an AI chat** (upload the avatar image and the product image):
> "Give me a detailed image prompt where the avatar is holding the product towards the camera, making sure the product label is sharp, readable and consistent. Include all text that is on the label in the prompt and ensure the product proportions are accurate." Then add location, lighting and other details if wanted.

**To swap a product into an inspiration image** (upload the inspiration and your product):
> "Give me a highly detailed prompt to put the [MY PRODUCT] bottle into the scene instead of the [ORIGINAL PRODUCT]. Include details of all of the text and make it as accurate as possible. Also change [the features / props, e.g. peaches → mangos] and change the background colour to match."

To avoid copying, change the person's appearance or upload your own avatar reference. When recreating, put your own spin on it and **apply the advanced prompting lessons** (add a foreground layer, better motivated lighting, a clear camera choice) so the result beats the original.

**Clothing swaps:** send the model image and the garment image to an AI chat, ask for the exact editing prompt, then generate with both images as references. Afterwards you can prompt new camera angles (side view, zoomed-out studio view) or add accessories.

If proportions keep going wrong, add **multiple product references with size comparisons** (the product next to a hand, a phone, a mug).

## Label-lock template (product held toward the camera)

This structure comes from the course's before/after community example (blurry gibberish label → perfect label). Fill the brackets and keep the section order.

```
Ultra-realistic lifestyle product photograph. The [person] from the reference image is [standing/sitting] in [specific real location], [real light source, e.g. soft natural daylight from a window off-camera]. The environment feels authentic and lived-in, not staged.

PRODUCT PLACEMENT: [he/she] holds the [product type] directly toward the camera at chest height, fingers naturally wrapped around the lower half so the label is not covered. The product is the primary focal point, slightly closer to the camera than the face, so it is large and legible. Natural grip pressure, realistic hand skin texture, correct number of fingers. No motion blur.

LABEL: the label is flat to camera, perfectly aligned and fully readable. ALL TEXT MUST BE EXACT, SHARP AND SPELLED CORRECTLY. NO ALTERATIONS.
Product details (must match exactly):
- Brand name: "[BRAND]" ([position, e.g. vertical on the left])
- Main product name: "[NAME]" ([typography, e.g. large bold])
- Secondary text: "[LINE]", "[LINE]"
- Feature/claim lines: "[LINE]", "[LINE]"
- Packaging: [material/finish, e.g. matte white bottle], [colours of typography], [cap/lid]
- Size: [e.g. fits in one hand, about the height of a phone]
No glare or reflections over the text, no warped label, no incorrect fonts.

LIGHTING: soft key light illuminating the label evenly, gentle fill light preserving contrast, subtle rim light outlining the product shape. Natural daylight tones, no harsh shadows on the label.

FOCUS HIERARCHY: product and hand razor sharp; face slightly softer but still clear; background softly blurred.
(UGC/phone mode instead: product and face both sharp, background slightly soft but recognisable, phone-camera look.)

PERSON: [clothing, e.g. plain neutral t-shirt], natural and trustworthy, calm confident expression. No exaggerated posing, no influencer clichés, no fake smiles.

CAMERA: [50mm lens look, eye-level] (or: iPhone front camera at arm's length for UGC). Photorealistic skin tones, true-to-life colour.

No text distortion, no missing words, no branding changes, no extra logos, no watermark.
```

**Why it works:** the label text is written out line by line, the product is physically placed closer to the lens and flat to camera, the focus order is explicit, and the product gets its own light (key + fill + rim) so the text is evenly lit.

**Check before animating:** label spelling, label not curved/warped, scale against the hand, finger count and grip, product lit from the same side as the face.
