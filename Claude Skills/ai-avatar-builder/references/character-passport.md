## STEP 1: BUILD THE CAST CHARACTER PASSPORTS

Before a single frame exists, every character is locked into one image, the character sheet. That image is the passport: the same person from every angle, in the same clothes, under the same light. From then on the passport is the character. It goes into every shot as a reference, and the model is left with nothing to invent.

FILL TWO SLOTS

CHARACTER

Age, build, height. Ethnicity. Hair colour, length and how it sits. Facial hair. Skin tone and texture. Tattoos and exactly where they fall on the body. Scars, piercings, teeth. Default expression.

### WARDROBE

Every garment by name, colour and material. Logos and their exact placement: chest, back, sleeve. Footwear. Jewellery. Headwear. Eyewear. When the shape of a garment matters, describe the shape, not the brand.

THEN LOCK DOWN

### WARDROBE

Same garments, same colours, same logo placement in every shot of the film.

### HAIR

Same length, same parting, same styling or tie-back.

### SKIN

Tattoos in the same places, at the same size, in the same colours.

### JEWELLERY

Chains, rings, earrings. Either present in every shot or in none.

### EYEWEAR AND HEADWEAR

Decide once whether the character wears it. On in one shot and off in the next breaks the film faster than anything else.

### PROPORTIONS

Build and height never change. Left alone, the model makes everyone slimmer and taller.

## STEP 2: LOCATIONS

The look of the film is decided here. Not in the character shots and not in the grade, but in the location: film stock, hour of day and sun direction are set once. That is why this section opens with the look block, copied into every location without a single edit and carried on into the shot prompts, every time. A location is a place, not a picture. The moment the camera moves, the model invents everything again unless you tell it where it now stands relative to the last view.

### FILL THREE SLOTS

#### WHERE THE CAMERA STANDS

Not "a view of the street" but a camera position: deep in the cul-de-sac, at the kerb, in the middle of the road. The model puts the camera exactly where your words put it.

#### WHICH WAY IT FACES

Direction matters more than content. If the camera turned 180 degrees, write that.

### THE SUN

Height and direction. If shadows fall to the right in one frame, they fall to the right in all of them.

### TIME OF DAY

One hour for the whole location. Golden hour does not last forty minutes in life, but it does in a film.

### CAMERA HEIGHT

Eye level by default. Change it only when it is a decision, not an accident.

### LENS

One focal length per location. A different focal length reads as a different place.

### MATERIALS

Stucco, asphalt, chain-link, dry grass. List them once and repeat them verbatim.

### SKY

Colour and condition. Clouds appearing break a location harder than a moved building.

### EMPTINESS

Not one accidental person. People appear only where the story puts them.

Guys, think like a cinematographer on real film-set rather than someone ordering a picture. A cinematographer never says "show me the street"; he says "camera is here, facing that way, the exit is behind us".
