# Engagement tactics

Comments are the signal that tells a platform a video is alive, and early comments matter disproportionately - the first one to two hours after posting is the window where engagement decides how wide the video travels.

The psychology underneath all of this: most people will not be the first to comment. They scroll past unless they can see a conversation already happening. Once they can, they join.

## Work the first hour

Set a timer for sixty minutes after posting and treat engagement as the job.

- Reply to **every** comment, instantly - even "🔥" or "lol".
- Do not reply "thanks". Ask a follow-up, react with a twist, make a joke, say something slightly unexpected. The aim is to turn one comment into three or four.
- Build micro-conversations all over the comment section rather than a row of one-line replies.
- If someone is negative, engage anyway - respectfully, and at length. Others join in, and the platform counts all of it.
- Post the video to your story at the same time.

A comment section worked properly shows seventy comments from twenty commenters. The algorithm reads activity, not unique participants.

## Comment bait

Put something in the video that people cannot resist pointing out. This is the most reliable organic engagement device there is, because it uses the viewer's own impulse to correct or notice.

**Low-risk versions, which is what to use:**

- An object that does not belong: a random giraffe in the background for a split second, a modern logo in a historical scene, a kettle in a medieval kitchen.
- A deliberately odd styling choice that people will argue about.
- An opinion stated slightly more strongly than you hold it, inviting disagreement.
- An open question in the video or caption that splits opinion.
- Something half-visible that makes people ask what it was.

**Keep it believable.** If the bait is too obvious it reads as bait and puts people off; the goal is to just barely trigger the urge to comment.

**One caution worth stating.** The course also suggests deliberate factual errors - wrong dates, misattributed quotes, a planet named wrong - because corrections flood in. It works, but you are publishing something false to an audience that may believe it, and on educational or history content that reputation damage outlasts the view spike. Visual and opinion bait produce the same engagement without that cost, so use those instead.

## Keyword filtering

In TikTok's Settings and Privacy, under Comments, there is **Filter keywords**. Add the words and phrases your audience uses most - especially the negative ones, the repetitive catchphrases, and anything that undercuts the content.

The classic use for AI content: however real it looks, someone will zoom in, find a flaw and comment "this is AI", and once that comment is visible others pile on and the illusion breaks for everyone.

Why it helps reach rather than hurting it: filtered comments still count toward engagement, they are just hidden from public view unless you approve them. So each person who thinks they are the first to spot it comments - unaware fifty others already did - and the repetition feeds distribution.

Use it for the comments that break the experience, not for all criticism. An account with no visible disagreement looks managed, and disagreement is engagement too.

## Creator pods

Make genuine friends on the platform in your niche and agree to comment on each other's posts when they go up. Ten people doing this means ten real comments within minutes of posting - which is exactly the early signal the algorithm is looking for.

This works and carries no risk, because the accounts are real people with real opinions. It is also the most durable version of early engagement, since it keeps working after any individual tactic stops.

## Reply technique

- **Full sentences and questions outperform emoji replies.** Platforms favour replies that drive further interaction, and "🔥" drives none.
- **Pin the comment that frames the video well** - it shapes how everyone else reads it.
- **Reply to the question everyone is asking** in the main comment section, not just to that person.
- **Use replies as content.** A reply-video to a good comment is a free idea with built-in context.
- **Watch the language you reply in.** Replying in another language teaches the platform you have an audience there, and it will push your videos to that audience - which matters if you are targeting a specific market.

## Tactics that carry real risk

The course describes a manufactured-engagement method: post, wait, then comment from a second device or unlinked account with a divisive question, reply from your main account, and argue with yourself until real people join.

It does work, for exactly the reason stated at the top - people join conversations rather than starting them. It is also a direct violation of platform terms on inauthentic engagement, and the consequences land on the account rather than the tactic: suppressed reach, or loss of the account, usually without explanation or appeal. Accounts built on it are also brittle, because the tactic has to keep running forever.

The legitimate substitutes get most of the same effect:

| Instead of | Do this |
|---|---|
| Fake first comment | A pinned comment from your own account that poses the divisive question openly |
| Arguing with yourself | A genuinely debatable opinion in the video, then arguing with real repliers |
| Manufactured volume | A creator pod of real accounts |
| Fake controversy | Comment bait that is actually in the video |

Same mechanism - visible conversation early - without betting the account on it.

## Signals beyond comments

- **Watch time** is still the dominant signal. No amount of comment work saves a video people leave at two seconds.
- **Shares and saves** weigh heavily. Content worth saving is practical, listed or reference-like; content worth sharing is surprising or socially useful.
- **Follows from a video** tell the platform the account is worth recommending - which is a bio and thumbnail problem as much as a content one.
- **Your own viewing** trains the audience model. Spend ten to fifteen minutes a day liking, commenting and watching in your niche, and never watch outside it on the account's device. If you want to scroll for fun, use a different account.
- **Rewatches** are the cheapest signal to engineer - see the loop devices in `hooks-and-retention.md`.
