# Platform, branding and posting

## Choosing a platform

Pick one primary platform to learn, then cross-post everywhere. Learning three algorithms at once means learning none.

| Platform | Suits | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | Fast discovery, trends, experimentation | Hardest-working algorithm for new accounts; strictest about niche consistency and content rules |
| Instagram Reels | Brand building, conversion to profile and DMs | Audience converts better to offers; grid and profile matter more |
| YouTube Shorts | Long-term compounding, search-adjacent discovery | Content keeps earning views for longer; strong feeder into long-form |

Decide by where your offer or ambition lives, not by which is easiest. Then produce once and post to all three - the hard part was making the video, and uploading the same asset everywhere takes minutes and doubles or triples reach.

## Geography as a lever

Platforms infer where your audience should be from your content, your language, your hashtags and your own location signals. If you want a particular market - commonly the US, for earnings per thousand views - then make content about that market, reference it, and tag it accordingly.

The flip side is that these signals are easy to break by accident:

- Logging into the account from another country shifts the inferred audience, and the recovery can be slow. If you travel, do not log in on that device.
- Replying to comments in another language teaches the platform you have an audience there, and it will push future videos to that audience.
- Logging into someone else's account on your phone, or yours on theirs, is the fastest way to get flagged outright.

## Channel branding

One-time decisions that compound every post:

- **Handle and name** - readable, searchable, niche-adjacent.
- **Bio** - clear and specific, a reason to follow, and a line of personality. Emojis help readability. A visible follower goal ("6,500 / 7,000") works because people like feeling their follow counts for something.
- **Thumbnail system** - the same treatment on every post, so the grid reads as one body of work. This is the main thing that turns a visitor into a follower.
- **Profile picture** - legible at the size it is actually displayed.
- **Pinned posts** - your three best or three most representative.
- **Link** - one destination, matching whatever your content promises.

## Upload settings

| Setting | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution and frame rate | 1080p at 30fps | Platforms re-encode aggressively; 4K60 uploads get crushed and look worse |
| Aspect ratio | 9:16, full frame | Letterboxing reads as repurposed content |
| Safe area | Keep faces, products and text in the middle band | Captions sit at the bottom, buttons on the right |
| Captions | On, short lines, high contrast | If people are reading, they are still watching |
| Hashtags | Four maximum | More confuses the audience model |
| Sound | Original audio, or a trending sound if the format needs it | Copyrighted music on videos over a minute risks an unoriginal-content flag; under a minute is usually tolerated |
| AI disclosure | Tick it | Many large accounts never have and still get millions of views, so it does not appear to suppress reach - but the downside risk of not disclosing is account-level |
| Cover frame | Chosen deliberately, matching your thumbnail system | It is the grid, not just the video |

## Cadence

- **3-5 posts a day maximum.** Beyond that, performance drops.
- **At least 1-2 hours between posts.** Posting twice within an hour visibly costs views.
- **Same time every day.** This is less about finding the magic hour and more about making your metrics comparable: if posting time is a variable, you cannot read anything else.
- **Ride momentum.** When a video takes off, post again soon. Viewers on the hit will see the new post, and that is the cheapest distribution available.
- **Have a buffer.** Two or three finished videos in reserve means a bad day does not break the streak - and a clone or a batched set makes this easy (`ai-clone-yourself`, `ai-creator-workflow/references/batching.md`).

## Captions and text

- Text on screen keeps people watching, because reading is watching.
- Put the hook in text as well as in the audio - a large share of viewers start muted.
- Keep lines to two or three words, and out of the caption and button zones.
- The written caption should add something - a question, a detail, a reason to comment - not repeat the video.

## Cross-posting

Post the same asset to every platform, with small adjustments:

- Remove any platform-specific watermark, which suppresses reach elsewhere.
- Re-write the caption and hashtags per platform.
- Re-check the safe area; UI overlays sit differently.
- Adjust pacing if you have the time - each platform's audience tolerates setup differently - but do not let this stop you posting.

Then screenshot the video and post it to your story on each platform. It costs seconds and sends an extra engagement signal to people who already follow you.

## Trends

- Watch your niche daily. When something performs, make your version with a few changes and post it fast - same day if possible. The window is short.
- Bookmark and save video ideas as you scroll. On a day when you have no ideas, your bookmarks are the plan.
- Do not try to reinvent a working format. Volume of attempts at proven formats beats originality for growth; save originality for when you have an audience to be original for.
