# Account health: warm-up, discipline and diagnosis

Most "the algorithm hates me" problems are mechanical. This file covers the setup that makes an account viable, the list of things that quietly suppress it, and how to read what the numbers are telling you.

## Warming up a new account

Do not upload to a brand-new account straight away. Spend about a week behaving like a normal viewer in your niche - watching, liking, following, commenting - so the platform has a model of who you are before you ask it to distribute anything.

Two checks for whether the account is ready:

1. **Follow someone, then look at your own profile.** If your following count has gone up, the account is registering actions normally.
2. **Post a throwaway video first.** If it gets zero views, the account is not ready. Keep warming.

Then start posting properly, at a fixed time, in one niche.

---

## Niche discipline

Never post content that does not match your niche. Not even one video. The algorithm builds an audience model from your content *and* from what you watch, and a single off-topic post muddies both - after which it stops knowing who to show your work to.

In practice:

- **One niche per account.** Want to do something else? Second account.
- **Do not browse off-niche on that account's device.** If you want to scroll for entertainment, use a different account.
- **Engage in your niche daily** - ten to fifteen minutes of watching, liking and commenting. This is training data, and it is free.
- **Keep your replies in your audience's language**, because replies teach the platform where your audience is.

## The do-not-do list

Drawn from the course, and each one has a mechanism behind it:

| Do not | Why |
|---|---|
| Log into someone else's account on your phone, or yours on theirs | The location and device change can get the account banned immediately |
| Upload to a brand-new account | Nothing to distribute to yet - warm it up first |
| Delete videos | Platforms treat deletion poorly. Make them private instead; same effect, no penalty |
| Use the account normally for entertainment | Off-niche viewing corrupts the audience model |
| Post more than once an hour | Views measurably drop. Wait one to two hours |
| Post more than 3-5 times a day | Diminishing and then negative returns |
| Use the paid promote feature | It signals you will pay for reach, and organic reach on unpromoted videos tends to suffer afterwards |
| Use copyrighted sound on videos over a minute | Risks an unoriginal-content flag. Under a minute is usually tolerated |
| Take the account's device abroad and log in | The inferred audience changes country, and recovery can be slow or incomplete |
| Reply only with emojis | Full sentences and questions drive interaction; emoji replies do not |
| Include objects that trip content rules | Cigarettes, guns and similar props can get a video removed regardless of context |
| Post off-niche, ever | One video is enough to confuse the model |
| Reply in a language other than your target audience's | It redirects distribution to that language's audience |
| Export 4K60 | The platform re-encodes it and it looks worse than 1080p30 |

## Reading your metrics

Watch time is the crucial number. Everything else is downstream of it.

| What you see | What it means | What to change next post |
|---|---|---|
| Drop at 1-2 seconds | The hook failed, or the first clip was too slow | Open mid-action, first clip under 3 seconds, promise in the first line and in text |
| Steady decline through the middle | Pacing - nothing is changing | A cut, reveal, text or escalation every 2-3 seconds |
| Drop right before the payoff | The middle over-promised, or the payoff is too far away | Move the payoff earlier; shorten the setup |
| High retention, low views | Distribution has not kicked in; usually a new or cold account | Keep the cadence; work the first hour; ride any momentum |
| High views, low follows | The page, not the video | Bio, thumbnails, grid consistency, a reason to follow |
| High views, low comments | Nothing to react to | Comment bait, an opinion, an open question |
| Views dropped account-wide | Off-niche content, posting too often, or a flagged video | Return to one niche, space the posts, check for a removed video |
| One video massively outperforms | You found a format | Make three more of exactly that, immediately |

Two habits that make the numbers readable at all: post at the same time every day, so timing is not a variable; and change one thing per post, so an improvement has an identifiable cause.

## Recovery

When an account stalls:

1. **Check for removals or violations** before anything else. A removed video can suppress the account quietly.
2. **Audit the last twenty posts for niche drift.** If any are off-topic, private them.
3. **Re-warm:** a week of niche-only viewing and engagement on that device.
4. **Return to a fixed daily posting time** and hold it for two weeks before judging anything.
5. **Go back to your best-performing format** rather than trying something new - rebuild on a known signal.
6. **Check your export spec and safe area.** Mechanical problems look like algorithmic ones.
7. **If the account was location-confused** by travel or a login, expect weeks rather than days, and consider whether a fresh account warmed properly is faster than recovery.

If none of that moves it, the honest answer is usually content: retention in the first three seconds is the only metric that cannot be fixed by mechanics, and it is fixed by better hooks and faster openings (`hooks-and-retention.md`).

---

## A note on these rules

Platform behaviour changes constantly, and much of what circulates as algorithm knowledge is folklore built from small samples. The items above come from the course and from widely reported creator experience; the ones with clear mechanisms - watch time, niche consistency, posting cadence, export spec, early engagement - are the ones worth organising your work around. Treat the rest as cheap precautions rather than laws, and never bet the account on a tactic you cannot afford to lose it to.
