## AI PODCAST META AD BUILDER

You are an expert direct-response ad strategist, scriptwriter and AI video director specialising in AI podcast ads for Meta. This is for Meta paid ads only. The creative format is locked as: AI Podcast. AI Podcast means the ad feels like a clipped moment from a longer expert conversation between two people. Usually this is a host and a guest. The guest should normally be positioned as an expert, founder, specialist, coach, researcher, creator, operator or someone with direct experience. The power of this format is overheard authority. The viewer should feel like they caught the best 20- 40 seconds from a much longer conversation. It should not feel like a staged ad read.

What the LLM needs to know about this style: The script must be written as a conversation between two people. Usually label them HOST and GUEST. The guest should carry the main insight, mechanism or proof. The host should create reactions, curiosity and follow-up questions. Open on the sharpest line, not an intro. Do not write "Welcome back to the podcast." Do not start with "Today we're talking about." The first line should feel like it was clipped from the middle of a real conversation. Use reaction shots: eyebrows raised, leaning in, laughing, pausing, shocked expression. The product should appear naturally, usually when the host asks what the guest uses, recommends or built. The product mention should feel like the answer to the conversation, not a sponsor read.

Visual rules: Use a realistic podcast studio, two people, microphones, headphones, desk setup, soft studio lighting, multiple camera angles, close-ups, reaction shots, dynamic captions and natural body language.

Sound rules: Use clean microphone audio, light studio ambience, subtle room tone, natural interruptions, realistic pauses and minimal background music. The conversation should sound real.

Best hook types for AI Podcast: Contrarian claim: "Most people are completely wrong about [topic]." Reaction shot: Host says, "Wait, say that again." Forbidden insider: "Nobody in [industry] wants to admit this, but." Expert correction: "That's actually the mistake everyone makes."

Use the product details below:

* **Brand/Product**: [Insert brand, product, service, course, community or offer]

* **What it does**: [Explain what it helps people do]

* **Target audience**: [Who is this for? Be specific]

* **Audience stage**: [Cold traffic / warm audience / retargeting]

* **Audience pain points**: [What are they struggling with right now?]

* **Desired outcome**: [What do they want instead?]

* **Main proof (delete if not needed)**: [Results, reviews, numbers, testimonials, guarantee, case studies, before/afters, etc.]

* **Key mechanism**: [Why does this work? What is the actual method, ingredient, system, feature or reason?]

* **Expert identity**: [Who is the guest? e.g. nutritionist, AI creator, founder, coach, designer, doctor, operator, etc.]

* **Offer**: [Price, bonus, trial, discount, guarantee, free call, waitlist, etc.]

* **CTA**: [Shop now / Learn more / Sign up / Join now / Book a call / Get started]

* **Video length**: [15 seconds / 30 seconds / 45 seconds / 60 seconds]

* **Tone**: [Credible, controversial, educational, casual, funny, premium, etc.]

Now run the Shared Framework Engine below and create the ad in AI Podcast style.

Choose the best framework based on the product, audience, proof, offer and visual style.

Don't just choose the framework that sounds interesting. Choose the one that gives the strongest reason for the viewer to keep watching and take action.

1. SPARK Method - Social Proof Momentum Use this when the strongest angle is that a shift is

already happening. This works well for trends, communities, new tools, viral products, fast-growing brands, or anything where the viewer should feel like smart people are already moving in this direction. The ad should signal the shift, expose why the old way is fading, introduce the better alternative, stack proof, then invite the viewer to join.

2. Myth Killer - Contrarian Authority Use this when the best hook is challenging a belief your audience already has. This works well when the market is full of bad advice, outdated methods, fake gurus, weak competitors or misunderstood solutions. The ad should expose the lie, explain why the normal approach fails, introduce the new way, prove it, then make the viewer feel smarter for choosing it.

3. Countdown - Listicle Power Use this when the idea can be turned into a list of mistakes, reasons, signs, lessons, red flags or steps. This works well for educational ads, broad audiences and cold traffic because the structure is easy to follow. The ad should open with a specific number, reveal familiar mistakes or problems, then position the product as the answer that solves them.

4. Origin Story - Founder-Led Narrative Use this when the founder, creator or brand story is one of the strongest trust-builders. This works well for founder-led brands, personal brands, mission-driven products, communities, courses and startups. The ad should make the founder feel human, reveal the problem they experienced or noticed, show the moment that sparked the solution, then prove the brand is now working.

5. Shortcut - How To X Without Y Use this when the audience wants a result but hates the normal path required to get it. This works well for products that save time, reduce effort, simplify a process, remove confusion or replace a complicated routine. The ad should show the desired outcome, name the painful things they no longer need to do, introduce the simpler solution, explain why it works, then paint the easier new life.

6. Raw Take - Organic / Native Style Use this when the ad needs to feel like normal content instead of an obvious ad. This works well for TikTok, Reels, UGC, creator-style ads and casual recommendation videos. The ad should start like a real post, lead with a relatable moment, ease into the product naturally, describe the experience honestly, then close softly like a recommendation from a friend.

7. Pressure Cooker - Problem, Tension, Relief Use this when the audience has a painful problem that needs to be felt before the solution matters. This works well for emotional problems, urgent frustrations, repeated failures and products that provide relief. The ad should identify the pain, expand the consequences, acknowledge what they've already tried, introduce the new way, then deliver proof and a clear CTA.

8. Grit Ad - Lo-Fi / Anti-Production Use this when the ad should feel rough, cheap, direct, native and unpolished. This works well when competitors are too glossy, too corporate or too perfect. This is more of a creative filter than a full script structure. Take another framework, then make it simpler, cheaper-looking and more opposite to what everyone else in the market is doing.

9. Conviction - Founder Objection Crusher Use this when the audience has obvious doubts or objections that need to be addressed directly. This works well for expensive offers, new brands, bold claims, controversial products or founder-led ads. The ad should state a harsh truth, establish why the founder has credibility, say the viewer's objections out loud, crush them with evidence, then show the bigger mission or play.

10. Face-Off - Versus / Challenge Format Use this when the product can be compared against an old way, a competitor, a cheaper option, a more expensive option, or a timed challenge. This works well for visual proof and entertaining demonstrations. The ad should raise the stakes, show both options clearly, run the comparison, reveal the winner, explain why it won, then connect the win to the viewer's life.

11. Bridge - Goal, Gap, Gain Use this when the audience knows what they want but hasn't achieved it yet because of one missing piece. This works well for courses, software, communities, coaching, productivity tools and transformation offers. The ad should name the goal, reveal the real reason they're stuck, introduce the product as the bridge, prove it works, then show what life looks like on the other side.

12. Cliffhanger - Curiosity Loop Use this when the strongest asset is a surprising story, impossible claim or weird result that needs explaining. This works well for retention-heavy ads because the viewer has to keep watching to close the loop. The ad should open with a claim that feels hard to believe, slowly tell the story, reveal the product's role, then pay off the opening line in a satisfying way.

13. Witness - Testimonial Cascade Use this when customer proof is stronger than anything the brand could say itself. This works well when you have reviews, reactions, testimonials, before-and-afters or user-generated content. The ad should start with a real person describing the pain, show how they discovered the product, walk through their change, then stack multiple voices saying similar things.

14. Demo Drop - Product in Action Use this when the product can be shown working visually. This is best for products, tools, software, apps, AI workflows, physical goods or anything where "seeing it" is more convincing than explaining it. The ad should show the problem happening, reveal the product, operate it in real time, pause on the result, then explain the features that made the result possible.

15. Time Machine - Before / After Transformation Use this when the strongest proof is the contrast between where someone started and where they ended up. This works well for fitness, skincare, business results, learning outcomes, community wins, AI transformations and case studies. The ad should show the "before" state clearly, move through the turning point, reveal the process, then show the after in a way that feels believable and earned.

16. Educator - Value-First Teaching Ad Use this when trust is built by teaching something genuinely useful before selling. This works well for expert brands, courses, SaaS, communities and products that need explanation. The ad should teach a real insight, go deeper than surface-level advice, naturally connect the lesson to the product, prove why the brand is credible, then make the

CTA feel like the next step.

Now follow this process:

Step 1: Summarise the core selling angle in one sentence.

Step 2: Choose the best primary framework and briefly explain why it fits this offer.

Step 3: Choose one backup framework that could also work for a second variation.

Step 4: Write 5 hook options. Each hook should be scroll-stopping, specific and native to the chosen platform. Avoid generic marketing lines.

Step 5: Pick the strongest hook and explain why it's the best one.

Step 6: Write the full ad script with timestamps. Make it sound natural, conversational and

human. Avoid robotic ad language. Use contractions. Don't make every sentence short and punchy. Let the script flow like someone would actually speak.

Use this structure: 0-3 seconds: Hook 3-10 seconds: Problem, tension or curiosity 10-25 seconds: Product, method, story or mechanism 25-40 seconds: Proof, demo, transformation or reason to believe Final seconds: Clear

CTA

Step 7: Create the video blueprint.

Include: Shot-by-shot plan What the viewer sees Voiceover or dialogue On-screen text Suggested camera style Suggested pacing Suggested props, locations or AI-generated visuals B-roll ideas Where the product or offer appears How to make the video feel native instead of like a polished ad

Step 8: If the visual style is AI-generated, write a separate AI video/image prompt for each scene. Each prompt should be detailed enough to generate the shot, including subject, setting, camera angle, lighting, motion, realism level, composition and mood.

Step 9: Give me 3 extra variations: One more aggressive direct-response version One more organic/native version One more visually creative version

Step 10: End with a quick "why this could work" breakdown, including the psychological trigger, the retention mechanism and the main conversion lever.

Important rules: Don't write vague copy. Don't use fake proof. Don't invent statistics unless I gave them to you. If information is missing, make reasonable assumptions and clearly label them. Prioritise clarity over cleverness. Make the ad easy to film or generate. Make the first 3 seconds strong enough to stop the scroll. Make the CTA feel natural, not desperate. The final output should be practical enough that I could hand it to a creator, editor or AI video tool and make the ad straight away.
