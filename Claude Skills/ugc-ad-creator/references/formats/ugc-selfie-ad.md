# Hook example (woman falling through ceiling):
PROMPT:
Create a hyper-realistic vertical 9:16 video that feels like raw, accidental TikTok phone footage. The camera is placed on the floor and remains there the entire time, angled slightly upward toward the ceiling, as if someone left their phone recording on the ground. The framing should feel imperfect and believable, with the ceiling dominating most of the shot and part of the floor visible in the foreground. Show a lived-in modern room — maybe a rug edge, coffee table leg, sofa corner, scattered clothes or cushions — but keep the focus on the ceiling area above.
The footage begins with 2–3 seconds of stillness, creating tension. Quiet room tone. Slight phone grain. Natural indoor daylight. No dramatic music.
Then the chaos starts suddenly and violently.
Instead of a neat fall, the ceiling should first show small stress cracks, then a sharp creaking snap, then a much bigger violent break. The woman should partially crash through unevenly, with one leg or shoulder breaking through first, followed by the rest of her body forcing the ceiling open in a messy, uncontrolled way. The plaster should not break in a clean circle — it should explode in jagged chunks, with dust, drywall fragments, insulation fluff, and debris flying everywhere.
Her body movement should feel awkward and out of control, not like a stunt performer or superhero. She should slam through badly, hit part of the broken ceiling on the way down, lose balance mid-fall, flail a little, and land hard and chaotically near the camera. She should not land gracefully. She should hit the floor in a clumsy, believable way — maybe shoulder-first, hip-first, or in a twisted half-roll — with pieces of plaster bouncing around her.
The impact should feel heavy and real:
the camera shakes hard
the phone slightly skids or jolts from the impact
debris hits near the lens
dust briefly clouds the image
autofocus struggles for a second
exposure shifts slightly
the sound momentarily clips from the crash
Important: make the fall feel like a captured accident, not a polished action sequence. It should feel surprising, messy, badly controlled, and absurdly real.
After landing, she should stay down for a beat in the rubble, surrounded by settling dust and broken plaster. She looks stunned for a second, coughs lightly, brushes dust off herself, then leorants toward the floor camera. Her hair is messy, her clothes are dusty, she looks slightly embarrassed but unharmed. She looks directly into the lens and says in a calm, deadpan, slightly breathless tone:
“It’s okay… I’m only AI.”
Her lip sync must be clear and natural.
After saying it, she gives a small awkward smile or tiny reassuring nod while dust continues falling in the background from the broken ceiling above.

## Extra teaching from the avatar-ad lesson videos (Phase 5)

**The pipeline (4 steps):** 1) generate the character image and prepare product photos; 2) script the ad with the master prompt above (paste it into an AI chat with the character reference and product images); 3) generate each clip in the video model with the character and product references; 4) stitch and edit, trimming out glitches (a hand going wrong, odd movement).

**Reference quality decides everything.** The quality of the reference photo is directly tied to the quality of the video. Before animating, ask: does it look real? Is the skin and lighting natural? Is it impossible to tell it's AI? If not, regenerate. Always give the video model a reference of the avatar; leaving the person to chance gives a much less realistic result.

**Products with packaging:** if the product opens, comes in a packet or has something inside, **upload a reference of the inside too** (e.g. the actual sweet, the pill, the cream texture) so the model knows what it looks like in use. Also say whether the label has text and describe what the product is/does so the avatar handles it correctly.

**UGC ad styles to choose from:** talking avatar only; talking + B-roll; fast-cut B-roll with voiceover; normal-cut B-roll; before/after transformation; day in the life; problem → solution (emotional storytelling, e.g. tired all the time until…); authority (e.g. a doctor or expert explaining how to use the product properly). Add the chosen style to the master prompt for a more specific ad.

**Dialogue length must match clip length.** Too many words → the voice speeds up dramatically or gets cut off. **Too few words for a long clip → the character tends to speak gibberish.** Aim for roughly 2.5 spoken words per second (≈12 words for 5s, ≈20 for 8s, ≈35-38 for 15s). For longer ads, ask the chat for Part 1, Part 2, Part 3… (15s each), generate each separately and stitch.

**Voice:** you can upload your own audio or a generated voice (e.g. ElevenLabs) and tag it in the prompt.

**Hooks:** stopping the scroll is the most important part. For Seedance, **visual-chaos hooks** (physical action, destruction, absurd moments played deadpan, like the woman falling through the ceiling) work especially well because the model is strong at physical action. Generate ideas with the hook template above and test them.

**Budget:** use the full-quality model for the hero ad, a faster/cheaper tier for tests. Cheaper lip-sync models generate short clips (e.g. 8s), so script in hook / body / CTA clips and stitch them.

**B-roll over talking clips** adds new camera angles and hides cuts: product in hand (handheld), product on the counter, applying/using it, close-up texture.
