# Framework 10: The Face-Off

Psychology, step breakdown, script template, example script and hook bank.

*Versus / Challenge Format*

### The Psychology

Competition is hardwired entertainment. From gladiator arenas to YouTube boxing matches, humans can't look away from a contest. The Face-Off leverages this by framing your product in a direct comparison, either against a competitor, against a difficult task, or against a clock. The stakes create tension, and tension creates watch time.

### Quick Reference: R.I.N.G.

R = Raise the Stakes
I = Initiate the Showdown
N = Name the Winner
G = Go Get Yours

### DETAILED BREAKDOWN

### R Raise the Stakes

Open with a challenge that feels fun, relatable, and slightly ridiculous. The stakes should be visual and immediately graspable.

**Example:**

"Can I deep clean my entire apartment before my UberEats arrives?"

"Speed test: our blender vs. a $500 professional grade one"

### I Initiate the Showdown

Show both options side by side. Split screen is king. Create a clear visual comparison that the viewer can follow.

**Example:**

[Split screen: Timer counting down / Product in action vs. competitor struggling]

### N Name the Winner

Reveal the result with energy. The winner should be obvious, satisfying, and slightly surprising in HOW much better it performed. Then explain WHY.

### Example:

"Done in 47 seconds. The other one? Still chunky. Not even close. It's the [feature], [technical explanation in simple terms]. That's the difference."

## G Go Get Yours

CTA that references the challenge. Tie the victory directly to the viewer's life.

### Example:

"If your blender can't keep up with your mornings, it's time for an upgrade."

## SCRIPT TEMPLATE

**HOOK**: [Raise the Stakes]
"[Fun, visual challenge]. Let's see what wins."
**BODY**:
[Initiate the Showdown] "On this side: [Product]. On this side: [Competitor/old way]. Ready? Go."
[Show the comparison, split screen, timer, visual side by side]
[Name the Winner] "[Product] wins by [dramatic margin]. Why? [Feature] = [simple explanation of why it's better]."
**CTA**: [Go Get Yours]
"If [relatable scenario where viewer needs this], [CTA that references the challenge]."

## EXAMPLE FULL SCRIPT

**Product**: TurboBlend Pro Blender

### HOOK (0-5 seconds):

[Person standing behind a counter with two blenders, frozen fruit, ice, and a timer visible]
"$89 blender vs. $500 blender. Frozen acai, frozen mango, ice, and almond milk. First one to make a smooth, drinkable bowl wins. Let's go."

### SHOWDOWN (5-25 seconds):

[Split screen. Both blenders loaded with the same ingredients at the same time. Timer starts.]
[Left side: TurboBlend Pro tears through everything immediately. Clean vortex. Smooth blend.]
[Right side: $500 blender struggles with the frozen acai. Chunks stuck to the sides. Person has to stop and push ingredients down with a tamper. Restarts.]
[Left side: Done. Timer shows 28 seconds. Pours into a bowl, smooth and thick.]

[Right side: Still going. Timer at 45 seconds. Still chunky.]

## WINNER (25-40 seconds):

[Shows both bowls side by side]

"28 seconds vs... well, it's been over a minute and there are still chunks. And this is the $500 one. Here's why. TurboBlend uses a 6-blade stacked system instead of the standard 4-blade. The bottom blades pull ingredients down while the top blades chop. Nothing escapes. The $500 blender uses brute force RPM, which sounds impressive but just creates an air pocket that pushes frozen stuff to the sides. More power doesn't mean better blending. Smarter blade design does."

## CTA (40-50 seconds):

"If your morning smoothie has chunks in it, it's not the recipe. It's the blender. TurboBlend Pro. $89 and it just beat a blender that costs 5 times more. Link below."
