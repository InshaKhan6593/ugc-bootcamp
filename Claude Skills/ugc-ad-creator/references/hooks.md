## Why The Hook Is Everything

*You have 1-2 seconds before they scroll*

The hook is the single most important part of any piece of content. It doesn't matter how good your video is if nobody watches past the first second. Your hook is the bouncer at the door. It decides whether people get in or walk past.

A great hook does one of three things: creates curiosity, triggers emotion, or promises value. That's it. Every viral video you've ever watched nailed at least one of these.

Below are 100+ hooks organized by category. Steal them, adapt them, make them your own.

# The Secrets Of TikTok Nobody Tells You

The Do's And Don'ts That Actually Matter

## Things You Should Do

1. Find your preferred posting time and then post at the same time every day. This way you can track metrics without posting time affecting them.

2. Comment bait. Add something annoying or random, or something that is completely out of place, into your video. People will point it out, increasing the number of comments and pushing you further into the algorithm.

3. Add a follower goal in your bio. For example, 6500/7000 🎯. People love to feel like they are making a difference, so they'll follow you.

4. Post 3-5 times a day MAXIMUM.

5. If you want a specific viewership demographic, e.g USA, you can make content focused around USA and TikTok will naturally push it to viewers there. Use hashtags like #USA #Newyork

6. Track your metrics, watch time is the most crucial factor, if you see people are scrolling away at 2 seconds, change your next video.

7. Make friends on the platform and message each other to comment on each other's post every time you post. If you get 10 people who do this, that is 10 comments on your video instantly.

8. Every time you post, screenshot the video and post it to your story too.

9. Use maximum 4 hashtags, anymore and the algorithm gets confused.

10. Add text to your videos, if people are reading, that means they are still watching.

11. Engage with videos similar to your niche. Like, comment and watch videos for at least 10-15 minutes a day. This way TikTok knows the sort of audience to push your content to.

12. Make your page look aesthetically pleasing by having similar thumbnails for each video.

13. Use momentum. If one of your videos is doing well, do not wait a few days to post again. Post frequently, this way the viewers on your other video will see your latest content too and it is more likely to go viral.

14. Have a clear and precise bio, with emojis in it.

15. Bookmark and save video ideas. If you run out of ideas, you can refer back to your bookmarks and copy someone's video.

16. Reply to every single comment on your videos. Even better, ask people a question in your reply. This will drive more engagement.

17. Make the first clip of your video no longer than 3 seconds. People want to see engaging content, if they have to wait 5 seconds for a scene change, they are probably going to scroll.

18. Make social media accounts on every platform. YouTube/insta/TikTok. If you have made the video already (the hard part), it is easy to spend a few extra minutes posting it EVERYWHERE. This way you don't limit yourself to one platform and can 2/3x your reach.

19. Follow trends. If you see someone in your niche posted a video YESTERDAY and it has 2million views. Recreate that exact video, with a few changes, and post it ASAP. Do not try to re-invent the wheel, just post content that you see is working for other people.

## Things You Should Not Do ✋

1. Log into someone elses account on your phone, the change of location will IP ban you immediately

2. Upload to a new account straight away – you need to warm the account up by watching content in your niche for at least 7 days. You can check to see if your account is warmed up by following someone, if you return to your account page and your number of followers has increased, you are good to go. Aswell as this make sure you upload a random video before posting content, if you get 0 views, your account is not ready yet.

3. NEVER delete videos, just private them. TikTok hates when videos are deleted.

4. You should not continue to use your TikTok account normally. If you want to watch videos, go on another account. Never watch videos outside of your niche.

5. Never post more than once within an hour. If you have posted, wait at least 1-2 hours before posting again. You will notice a decrease in views if you post too frequently.

6. DO NOT use the promote video feature. TikTok will see that you are happy to pay for views and limit the reach of any videos you do not promote in the future.

7. Do not use copyrighted sounds if your video is over 1 minute long. You can get away with it if it is shorter but never go over 1 minute. Your video will be flagged as unoriginal.

8. If you live, for example, in the UK, and you have 95% UK audience viewership, do NOT take your phone with you when you go on holiday. If you say go to Thailand, and log onto TikTok, TikTok now thinks you are from Thailand so will push your videos to the Thai community. You may never recover from this.

9. Do not over-reply with emojis only - while replies are good, TikTok favors replies with full sentences or questions—these drive more interaction than "🔥" or "❤️".

10. TikTok is extremely strict, so do not include any objects in your video that could get it removed. For example, having cigars or cigarettes on a table can have your video removed, or showing a gun.

11. Never post content that does not match your niche. Not even one video.

12. If you are trying to increase your RPM ($ per 1k views), and you need to get USA viewership for example, do not reply to comments that are in another language (e.g. Russian), this makes TikTok think that you have a Russian fanbase and pushes your video to more of this viewership.

13. Do NOT export from Capcut or upload videos in 4K resolution @ 60 fps. TikTok will immediately downgrade the quality. Upload TikTok's in 1080p 30fps. Trust me it will look better.

## TikTok Engagement Hacks

More Secrets The Guru's Don't Want You To Know\...

## Engagement Cheat Code 1

* Go to TikTok

* Tap "Settings and Privacy"

* Head to "Comments"

* Choose "Filter keywords"

In this section, start adding the phrases and words your audience comments most often—especially the negative ones, repetitive catchphrases, or anything that could hurt your brand's image.

Let's say you post hyper-realistic AI-generated content. No matter how real it looks, there's always that one person zooming in, hunting for flaws, and pointing out "this is AI." Once that happens, others join in and the illusion breaks. You want to get ahead of that.

Here's the genius part: TikTok will still count those filtered comments toward your engagement. They're just hidden from public view unless you approve them.

*Why does this boost your reach? Because when someone thinks they're the first to "expose" your content, they comment—unaware that 50 others already tried but were filtered. This repetition feeds the algorithm like wildfire and skyrockets your post's visibility. Most top creators use this hack, follow them!*

## Engagement Cheat Code 2

Here's one of the most underrated growth hacks I've personally used to go viral multiple times on TikTok — and the crazy part? It costs nothing.

### Step 1: Post your video and let it sit for 5–10 minutes

### Step 2: Grab a second phone (or use a different device or account not linked to your main one)

### Step 3: Head to your video and drop a comment — but don't make it random. Ask a question that creates a divide. Something opinion-based. Something that triggers people to take a side.

### Step 4: Switch back to your main account and reply to that comment — start debating. Literally argue with yourself if you have to. Stir the pot.

### Step 5: Once people start jumping in, keep it going. Reply to them from both accounts. Fuel the conversation. Fan the flames. You'll rack up 5–10+ comments in minutes.

*(Make sure you aren't connected to the same WiFi, use 4G if needed)*

This works like a charm. I've used it repeatedly — and I'm telling you, it never fails. The psychology behind it is simple: most people don't want to be the first to comment. They'll scroll past unless they see others already talking. People are way more likely to engage when they feel like they're joining an existing discussion instead of starting one from scratch.

By planting those early comments and sparking a mini-debate, you're hacking the algorithm's engagement signals. TikTok sees the activity — replies, likes, interactions — and flags your video as active. That signal can be the difference between a video that caps at 300 views and one that shoots past 10k+.

And the key is timing. TikTok gives you a short window (usually the first 1–2 hours) where early engagement matters a lot. That's your golden opportunity to get noticed — and this tactic gives you a way to manufacture momentum before the crowd arrives.

*Don't underestimate this. Start doing it consistently and watch what happens to your reach.*

# Engagement Cheat Code 3

Following on from the last cheatcode...

Most people upload their video... maybe reply to one or two comments... and then bounce. Big mistake.

What you should do instead: Treat every single comment like it's the start of a conversation — especially in the first 60 minutes.

Once you've posted your video and got the comments rolling in from your own commenting, set a 1-hour timer. During that window, your only job is to engage. Hard.

* The second someone comments — anything, even if it's "🔥" or "lol" — reply instantly.

* Don't just say "thanks." Turn it into a thread. Ask a follow-up. React with a twist. Make a joke. Say something unexpected.

* Repeat this with every comment. Your goal? Turn 1 comment into 3 or 4. Create tiny little micro-conversations all over your video.

* If someone's being negative? Even better. Keep it respectful, but go back and forth with them. Other people will jump in. You're feeding the beast.

*Think about it — when TikTok sees 20 people comment, but the comment count says 70, it's because you worked the section like a pro. The algorithm doesn't know who's who — it just sees engagement spiking early and starts pushing your content out wider.*

## Engagement Cheat Code 4

I've mentioned it earlier on in the course, but here's one of the sneakiest TikTok growth tricks that's worked for me more times than I can count:

***Make a small, subtle mistake on purpose, or include something that is so out of place that people cant help but correcting it.***

Yeah, seriously.

**Step 1: Post a video like normal — could be AI, educational, storytelling, opinion-based, anything**

**Step 2: Somewhere in the video (best option), text, speech, or captions, insert a "mistake" — just enough to trigger the viewer's urge to correct you**

**Step 3: Sit back and let the comments roll in as people try to "educate" you, little do they know that you are one step ahead**

This can be as simple as:

* Putting a Nike logo on a shoe, in a video thats meant to be in 1950
* Saying a quote wrong ("Einstein invented gravity")
* Messing up a date ("The internet was created in 1995")
* Misnaming something ("This is Saturn" when it's clearly Jupiter)
* Adding a random giraffe for a split second in the background
* Misspelling a basic word in a caption ("Your" instead of "you're")

Now, here's why this works like crack for engagement:

Most people scroll past videos without commenting. But the second they spot something "wrong," their ego kicks in. They have to say something. It's a reflex. They feel smart for noticing, so they drop a comment calling it out.

And when one person does it, others join in.

You just created a debate zone without needing to ask for anything. Every "correction" counts as a comment — even the snarky or rude ones. And TikTok doesn't care if the engagement is negative or positive. It just sees activity and pushes your video harder.

You can even double down:

* Respond to corrections with vague replies like "Are you sure?" or "That's what I read online" to stir it up more

* Like their comment but don't correct yourself in the caption — it keeps the loop going

Important: The "mistake" has to be believable. If it's too obvious or too fake, it looks like bait and might turn people off. The goal is to just barely trigger people into correcting you.

This strategy is especially powerful for educational or history-based content, storytelling, AI content, or "Did you know?" type videos.

You're basically using the audience's own ego to boost your content.

And once it starts? It snowballs. A video with 100 comments correcting your "error" is now getting boosted like crazy — all because you "accidentally" said Mars was the fourth planet from the sun instead of the third.

**Welcome to comment baiting, done smart.**
