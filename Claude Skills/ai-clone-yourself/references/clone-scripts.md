# Writing for a clone

Your clone is only as good as the inputs you give it. The chat model's job is to think, structure and multiply; the clone's job is to perform. If you still spend hours writing each script by hand, you have given back all the leverage cloning bought you.

## Treat the chat like a creative director

Do not say "write me a script". Give role, context and outcome:

> You are a social media strategist writing short-form video scripts for a realistic AI clone designed to feel human, conversational and confident.

That single framing improves tone, pacing and realism before you have described the topic - and realism matters more for clones than for anything else, because the delivery is synthetic and the writing is what compensates.

---

## The master system prompt

Lock this once and reuse it at the top of every scripting session. Fill all five slots:

> My AI clone is [who they are: confident, modern creator / technical founder / warm coach]. They speak [how: casually, short punchy sentences, avoiding corporate language]. My audience is [who: 30-year-old males interested in X / agency owners / new parents]. I sell [product or offer, or "nothing - this is personal brand content"]. My content should always [the non-negotiables: never hype, always one concrete takeaway, never longer than 30 seconds].

Save it. The more detail here, the less you fix later - and every script after this one starts from a brief instead of from zero.

## Ask for angles, not ideas

"Give me ideas" returns fluff. Ask for the thing that makes someone stop:

> Give me 20 short-form video ideas my AI clone could deliver directly to camera that feel controversial, insightful, or pattern-interrupting in the first 3 seconds. The hooks should be extremely strong.

> List 15 content ideas where my AI clone reveals something people do not realise about [topic].

> Give me 10 takes that would make someone in [audience] disagree out loud, while still being defensible.

Then filter hard. Two strong angles beat twenty average ones, because the strong ones get multiplied in the next step.

---

## The five-tone multiplier

Once one idea works, it becomes five scripts without another creative decision:

> Take this idea and rewrite it into 5 different short-form scripts:
> 1. Curious and subtle
> 2. Confident and bold
> 3. Slightly controversial
> 4. Educational but casual
> 5. Direct and blunt
>
> Each script should be under 30 seconds and sound natural when spoken aloud.

Add two pacing variants and two CTAs on top and one idea becomes a dozen posts. Test them, find the winner, then double down on what worked rather than inventing something new.

## Writing for the ear

The rule that governs everything: **if it would not sound normal said out loud, it will not lip-sync well.** Clones expose written-sounding language instantly.

> Write this script as if it is being spoken out loud by a real person. Use natural pauses, contractions, simple language and subtle emphasis. Avoid sounding like a script.

Then enforce mechanically:

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Short sentences, one idea each | Clauses stacked with "and", "which", "that" |
| Contractions - it's, you're, that's, I've | Full forms, which read as formal |
| Punctuation marking real pauses | Unpunctuated runs the engine reads flat |
| Concrete nouns and numbers | Abstractions and adjective piles |
| Words you actually say | "Utilise", "leverage", "furthermore", "in today's landscape" |
| One takeaway per video | Three points crammed into 25 seconds |

Budget around 2.5 spoken words per second. A 30-second script is roughly 75 words - far fewer than people expect, which is why most clone scripts need cutting rather than writing.

Read every script aloud before it goes to the clone. Anything that trips your tongue will trip the sync.

## Hooks that do not sound AI

The first two seconds decide whether the rest exists.

> Give me 10 opening lines my AI clone could say in the first 2 seconds that feel natural, slightly unexpected, and make someone stop scrolling.

The shape that works: conversational, slightly conspiratorial, implying there is something the viewer does not know.

- "This is going to sound fake... but it isn't."
- "People don't realise this yet."
- "No one warned me about this."
- "I was wrong about this for two years."
- "Everyone's doing this backwards."

Why these work for clones specifically: they are short, easy to say, and front-load curiosity before the viewer has had time to judge the face. A hook that needs four seconds of setup gives them time to notice the clone is a clone.

Hooks are also the cheapest thing to swap - rewrite the first two seconds and regenerate only that fragment, leaving the rest of the video intact.

## One script, every platform

> Take this script and adapt it for TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and a talking-head ad. Keep the core message the same, but adjust pacing and delivery for each.

Each platform has a different rhythm and a different tolerance for setup. Same message, different pacing, four assets from one decision.

## Batching a week

> Create a 7-day content plan for my AI clone. Each day should include: topic, hook, a 20-30 second script, and suggested tone.

Add to the output a B-roll column - what footage covers each video - and the week is production-ready, not just planned. Pair with the pass-based method in `ai-creator-workflow/references/batching.md`: write everything, generate everything, edit everything, in three sittings rather than seven.

## Refining an existing script

Short, specific asks work better than "improve this":

- "Make this sound more human and natural."
- "Reduce any robotic or formal phrasing."
- "Add subtle personality without adding length."
- "Make this feel like a real opinion, not a summary."
- "Cut this to 20 seconds of speech without losing the point."
- "Read this back and mark anything that would be awkward to say out loud."
- "Replace every abstraction with something concrete."

## The actual point

The goal is not to write more. It is to decide once and reuse forever. The chat thinks, structures and multiplies; the clone delivers, performs and scales. Together they remove filming from the process - but the deciding is still yours, and it is the only part that determines whether any of it is worth watching.
