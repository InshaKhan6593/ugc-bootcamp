# The capture recording

Everything a clone can ever do is decided in the few minutes of footage used to train it. No prompt fixes bad capture, and re-training later means re-recording, so treat this as the one session worth over-preparing for.

## Before you record

Read the platform's own instructions and watch its explainer video first. Each clone engine has quirks - required duration, whether to speak or stay silent, how much movement it wants - and following them beats following general advice.

Decide what the clone is *for* before framing anything, because the capture should match the finished content:

| Use case | Framing | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Short-form talking head | Chest-up, close, phone-ish distance | Conversational, slightly up-tempo |
| Explainer or course presenter | Mid-shot, a little more space | Measured, clear, steady |
| Ad spokesperson | Chest-up, direct to lens | Warm, confident, a touch faster |
| Corporate or internal | Mid-shot, seated or standing still | Calm, neutral, unhurried |

A clone captured for one of these can be pushed into another, but it will always look most natural doing what it was captured for.

## Light

- **Soft and front-biased.** A large diffused source in front of you, slightly above eye level. A window on an overcast day is excellent.
- **No hard shadow crossing the face**, and nothing cutting across one eye.
- **No backlight.** A bright window behind you is the most common ruined capture.
- **Even across the take.** Daylight that shifts during recording trains inconsistency. If the light changes, start again.
- **No colour cast** - mixed warm bulb and cool daylight gives you a face that cannot be colour-matched to anything later.
- **Catchlights in both eyes.** If the eyes look dull in the capture, they will look dull in every video.

## Camera and framing

- Eye level, not above or below. A clone captured looking up or down looks that way forever.
- Rock steady - tripod, stand or propped. Any drift in the capture becomes baked-in weirdness.
- Frame as the output will be framed, with a little room to crop.
- Sharp focus on the eyes. Check by zooming into the recording, not by looking at the screen.
- Highest sensible quality the engine accepts, without the look of a heavily processed phone mode. Turn off beauty filters, skin smoothing and portrait-mode background blur - all three confuse clone training.

## Background

- Plain and non-distracting, suiting every future video, not just this week's.
- Nothing with text, logos or a clock.
- Nothing that will date the content.
- Some depth helps - a few feet between you and the wall reads better than being flat against it.
- Consistent: if you ever re-capture, matching the background keeps older content compatible.

## Audio

Capture clean audio even if the clone is driven by a separate voice clone. It tells the engine your natural rhythm:

- Quiet room, soft furnishings, no fan, no traffic, no air conditioning.
- Microphone close and consistent - a lapel or a short-distance mic, not the camera's.
- No echo. A room with hard parallel walls needs something soft in it.
- Speak at the pace you want your content to have. The clone inherits your rhythm.

## Movement: the single most important adjustment

Do not overdo the hand movements. Some movement belongs in there, but a strong gesture, or a gesture repeated often, gets learned - and then the clone performs it far too often in every output, which reads as unmistakably fake.

Practical version:

- Small, natural movement: slight weight shifts, modest hand gestures low in frame, natural head movement.
- **Vary** the gestures. Never repeat the same one.
- No big sweeps, no pointing at the camera, no touching your face or hair.
- No leaning in and out of frame.
- Blink naturally. Do not stare.
- Keep your shoulders loose - tension reads as stiffness in every future video.

## Wardrobe

- Plain, mid-tone clothing. Avoid tight stripes and fine patterns, which flicker.
- Nothing white-hot or pure black - both lose detail and make matching harder.
- No big logos unless the clone is permanently branded.
- Simple jewellery, or none; moving earrings confuse the engine.
- Hair off the face, and styled the way you want it in every video from now on.

## What you say

Follow the engine's script if it gives one. If it does not:

- Speak naturally on a familiar topic rather than reading.
- Cover a range of expression - a serious stretch, a lighter stretch, a question, an emphatic point.
- Include real pauses.
- Avoid extremes: no shouting, no whispering, no laughing fits.

## After recording: review before training

Check the capture on a large screen before submitting it:

- Is focus sharp on the eyes throughout?
- Did the light stay constant?
- Any repeated gesture?
- Any moment where you leave frame or look away for long?
- Audio clean, level, no room echo?
- Is this framing and background something you will still want in six months?

Any "no" is a re-record. The session is short; the consequences are permanent.

## Re-record triggers later

- You change your look substantially - hair, facial hair, glasses.
- You want a different framing or setting as your standard.
- The engine ships a materially better training process.
- You keep fighting the same artefact in output; that is usually capture, not prompting.

Keep the original capture file archived. Engines improve, and being able to re-train from a known-good source is worth the storage.
