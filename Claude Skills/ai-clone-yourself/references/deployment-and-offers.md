## The jobs one clone covers

| Job | What it replaces |
|---|---|
| Daily short-form talking head | Setting up a camera every day for hooks, explanations and authority clips |
| Ad variations | Reshooting for a new hook, new pacing or a new CTA |
| Course lessons | A studio day, and re-recording when a lesson becomes wrong |
| Podcast clips | Having a face-to-camera version of audio-only content |
| Brand intros and outros | Re-recording every video when branding changes |
| A/B tests | Filming two versions to test tone, pace or length |
| FAQ and objection libraries | Writing the same answers in email a hundred times |
| Funnel videos | A presenter for welcome, offer explanation, pricing and objections |
| Localisation | Re-recording with a native speaker per market |
| Internal and training video | Booking the founder for onboarding and announcements |

## Advanced time savers

### No more camera days
You never set up lights, check framing, do retakes or work around a bad-hair, low-energy day. The clone is always well lit, on-brand, consistent and available. For most creators that alone is hours per week.

### Script to video in minutes
Write the text, generate the video. Rewrite the hook and regenerate only the first two seconds. Swap the CTA without touching the rest. The unit of work becomes a sentence instead of a shoot.

### Infinite versions from one idea
Three hook styles, two pacing styles, two emotional tones, two CTAs - twelve videos from one concept with nothing new recorded. This is also the fatigue-proofing mechanism for paid ads, where the hook burns out long before the offer does.

### The clone as your front end
Your face explains the offer, answers the FAQs, breaks down pricing, addresses objections and welcomes new users - working while you do not.

### Burnout protection
On a low-energy day the clone still posts, the ads still run and the brand still speaks. Consistency stops depending on how you feel, which is the actual reason most creators stall.

## Monetisation paths

Grouped by who pays and what they are buying.

### Selling your own presence
- **"AI twin" campaign work.** You become the face of several campaigns at once, with no reshoots and no scheduling. Brands pay for speed and consistency.
- **Retainer content.** Monthly packages, daily or weekly videos generated as your clone. Predictable recurring revenue.
- **A personal brand that never burns out.** Daily posting without daily filming - authority compounds while effort stays flat. More creators have cloned themselves than audiences realise.

### Selling the capability to others
- **Cloning founders for their ads.** Many founders are tired of being on camera. Clone them once and their ads never need their calendar again. High perceived value, clear transformation, highly scalable.
- **Done-for-you client cloning.** Set up and run clones for coaches, creators and executives.
- **Clone-based UGC for ecommerce.** Product demos, testimonial-style ads and explainers at volume, for brands that want real faces without booking creators. Volume and speed are what they are buying.
- **"Unlimited variations" packages.** One script, ten ad variations; weekly creative refreshes; fatigue-proof ad pipelines. This is what justifies a premium retainer.
- **Subscription spokesperson.** "Your AI spokesperson", "daily video content without filming", "always-on ad creator" - recurring, low-delivery-cost.
- **Corporate and internal training.** Onboarding, announcements, compliance explainers, company updates. Large budgets, and badly underserved.
- **Course and education production.** Build a course without going on camera, or produce courses for people who will not.
- **Localisation as a service.** Translate and localise founder videos, adjust accent and pacing, make region-specific versions. Extremely valuable to global brands.
- **Objection-handling libraries.** FAQ videos, "why choose us" clips, objection responses, video testimonials - sold as a package, a retainer, or a conversion-rate upgrade.
- **Founder presence without founder time.** Sold to busy founders, agencies and operators who hate filming. Position it as: you show up everywhere without being everywhere.

For any client work, get written permission to clone the person, and agree in writing what the clone may and may not say. That is both an ethical minimum and the thing that protects you when a campaign goes sideways.

## How to position any cloning offer

You are not selling AI videos, avatars or deepfakes. You are selling:

**speed, consistency, authority, scale, and presence without time cost.**

That is why people pay real money for it. Everybody wants their time back, and a founder who gets twenty times the content at the same effort is not comparing your price to a video editor's - they are comparing it to the value of the days they get back.

Practical implications for pricing and pitching:

- **Price on outcome, not production hours.** The second video costs you almost nothing, which is the point of the model; hourly pricing destroys the economics.
- **Lead with the time**, not the technology. "You never film again" lands; "we use an AI avatar platform" does not.
- **Show, do not explain.** A thirty-second clone video of the prospect themselves, made before the call, closes better than any deck.
- **Name the transformation.** From "I need to book a studio day" to "I send a message and the video exists."

## Running it as a service

### Onboarding a client
1. Permission and scope in writing - what the clone may say, where it may appear, who signs off.
2. A capture session, run to the checklist in `clone-capture.md`. Treat this as the one thing you cannot redo cheaply.
3. Voice samples and a locked delivery description.
4. A master system prompt for their voice and audience - see `clone-scripts.md`.
5. A first batch, delivered fast, to prove the loop.

### Delivery cadence
Write, generate, edit in three passes per batch rather than one video at a time. Keep a bank of approved B-roll per client so editing is assembly rather than sourcing. Keep every script, because approved scripts become templates.

### What to keep per client
The capture file, the voice samples and description, the master system prompt, approved B-roll, the script archive, and a log of which hooks performed. That package is the account's real value - and the reason a client stays.

### Where it goes wrong
- **Scope creep into strategy** without charging for it. Script writing *is* the work; price it in.
- **No approval step** on claims. A clone saying something the client did not approve is the one failure that ends a retainer.
- **Over-reliance on one clone format.** Mix B-roll, screen recordings and graphics, or the account's content all looks identical by month three.
- **Letting quality drift** because volume is easy. The gates in `ai-creator-workflow` apply here too.
